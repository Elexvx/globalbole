## Over 5,000 Diagnostic Tests Across Three Mobile Labs: Uganda and DRC Seal Kampala Health Security Roadmap

On November 9, 2026, the World Health Organization (WHO) Regional Office for Africa announced from Kampala that the governments of Uganda and the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) have agreed on an operational roadmap to accelerate their June 2026 cross-border health security Memorandum of Understanding (MoU), following a high-level bilateral meeting convened by WHO Regional Director for Africa Dr Mohamed Yakub Janabi[^1]. With communities and traders moving daily across their shared border, the Kampala accord establishes multi-tier coordination structures spanning national, provincial, district, and community levels to combat Ebola disease and other epidemic-prone pathogens[^1].

Field figures released at the Kampala meeting demonstrate tangible clinical and diagnostic capacity built since June 2026: Uganda has supported the establishment of three Ebola treatment units in the DRC health zones of Aru, Chomia, and Butembo, with a fourth facility currently under construction, while deploying three mobile laboratories to the DRC that have performed more than 5,000 diagnostic tests over the past three months[^1]. Ugandan Minister of Health Dr Chris Baryomunsi and DRC National Incident Manager and Presidential Taskforce Chair Prof Steve Ahuka confirmed that the two nations will institutionalize routine epidemiological data exchange, joint surveillance at points of entry, and collaborative evaluation of novel diagnostics and medical countermeasures[^1].

## Reclassifying Islet Cells from FDA Biologics to HRSA/OPTN Organ Transplants: US HHS Wraps Up Public Consultation

Alongside cross-border infectious disease coordination in East Africa, US biomedical regulators reached a key milestone in reviewing patient access to cellular therapies for chronic metabolic disease. On November 8, 2026, the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) closed the formal public comment window on their Request for Information (RFI), 'Improving Patient Access to Deceased Donor Islet Cells and Cell Products' (Docket No. FDA-2026-N-10738)[^2]. The initiative evaluates whether regulating deceased donor pancreatic islet cells under the FDA's biological product framework creates unnecessary barriers for patients with type 1 diabetes[^2].

Pancreatic islet clusters contain insulin-producing beta cells that, when isolated from a deceased donor pancreas and infused into a recipient, can restore endogenous insulin production and reduce or eliminate the need for daily insulin injections[^2]. Specifically, the HHS inquiry examines whether 'unmodified' deceased donor islet cells should be reclassified as human organs for transplantation rather than biological drug products—a shift that would transfer oversight of procurement, national waiting list allocation, and transplantation from the FDA to the Health Resources and Services Administration (HRSA) and the Organ Procurement and Transplantation Network (OPTN) while preserving patient safety standards[^2]. Both the Kampala cross-border health pact and the HHS islet cell regulatory review illustrate how health authorities are redesigning institutional frameworks to speed the delivery of life-saving diagnostics and cellular therapies[^1][^2].
