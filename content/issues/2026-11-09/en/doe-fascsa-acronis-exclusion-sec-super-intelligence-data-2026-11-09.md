## Targeting Persistent Root-Level Access Risks: DOE Enforces FASCSA Order #2026-02 to Remove Acronis Software

On November 9, 2026, the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) Office of Acquisition Management issued Policy Flash 2027-04 directing all DOE and National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA) contracting officers to enforce Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act (FASCSA) Exclusion and Removal Order #2026-02, issued by the Department of Homeland Security (Federal Register Docket DHS-2026-0694; FR Doc. 2026-22215)[^1]. The directive bars Acronis AG—a foreign, privately owned data protection provider with US subsidiaries—from all federal civilian executive branch information technology systems and procurements[^1].

The policy notice detailed the technical rationale established by the Federal Acquisition Security Council (FASC): software agents, backup engines, secure file-sharing tools, and disaster recovery solutions associated with Acronis require 'deep, persistent, and root-level administrative access across host infrastructure,' posing an unacceptable supply chain risk to US government information systems[^1]. Effective immediately, DOE offices are prohibited from entering into any contracts containing Acronis products or services and must purge them from agency-approved product lists; all existing Acronis covered articles must be identified, isolated, and completely removed from civilian agency systems by January 27, 2027, with any emergency mission waiver requiring formal endorsement by the DOE Chief Information Officer and General Counsel and personal authorization by the Secretary of Energy prior to DHS adjudication[^1].

## SEC Convenes Washington Roundtable on Machine-Understandable Data in the Age of Super Intelligence

On the same day that DOE moved to strip root-level third-party backup tools from federal networks, the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) hosted a full-day public roundtable at its Washington, D.C. headquarters on November 9, 2026 (9:00 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. EST), titled 'Machine-Understandable Data in the Age of Super Intelligence' (announced under Release No. 2026-106)[^2]. The forum brought together market participants, technologists, and financial regulators to evaluate how structured, machine-readable corporate disclosures are consumed by advanced AI and Super Intelligence (SI) systems, alongside the associated compliance costs and systemic risks[^2].

Joshua White, SEC Chief Economist and Director of the Division of Economic and Risk Analysis (DERA), emphasized that high-quality structured information is critical in the era of Super Intelligence and that industry feedback from the November 9 session will guide SEC staff on how the Commission can better facilitate trustworthy SI deployment across capital markets[^2]. Viewed together, the DOE FASCSA removal order and the SEC data governance roundtable highlight the dual trajectory of US federal technology oversight: enforcing strict zero-trust exclusion against foreign infrastructure software holding kernel- or root-level privileges while modernizing regulatory data schemas for autonomous machine reasoning[^1][^2].
