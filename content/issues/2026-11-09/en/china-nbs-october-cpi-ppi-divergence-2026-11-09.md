## Food Price Declines Offset Fuel and Consumer Hardware Increases as Core CPI Holds at 0.8%

Monthly price statistics published by the National Bureau of Statistics of China (NBS) on November 9, 2026, showed that the national Consumer Price Index (CPI) rose 0.7% year-on-year in October, easing by 0.2 percentage points from September, while month-on-month readings flattened to 0.0% after a 0.3% gain the prior month; over the January–October period, headline CPI averaged 0.9% higher than a year earlier[^1]. Core CPI, which excludes food and energy, increased 0.8% year-on-year, moderating by 0.2 percentage points from September and confirming that broad household demand inflation remains subdued[^1][^3].

Beneath the headline moderation, October's consumer price basket revealed a striking divergence between falling food costs and surging digital hardware prices. Food, tobacco, and alcohol prices fell 1.5% year-on-year, dragged down by steep declines in fresh vegetables (-16.9%), pork (-8.6%), and fresh fruit (-3.8%), whereas domestic gasoline prices jumped 22.4% year-on-year (up 4.2% month-on-month), lifting overall energy inflation to 9.9%[^1][^3]. Most notably, non-energy industrial consumer goods rose 1.2% year-on-year as higher upstream memory chip costs passed directly into retail electronics: prices for data storage devices, tablet computers, personal computers, and mobile phones surged 45.4%, 29.3%, 23.0%, and 16.7% year-on-year respectively, collectively adding roughly 0.26 percentage points to headline CPI growth, while gold jewelry prices fell 5.9% month-on-month and saw their annual gain narrow by 18.0 percentage points to 4.5%[^3].

## Factory-Gate Inflation Accelerates to 5.7% on Coal, Oil, and AI Computing Hardware Demand

In sharp contrast to restrained retail inflation, China's Producer Price Index (PPI) climbed 5.7% year-on-year in October, accelerating by 0.7 percentage points from September, while rising 0.7% month-on-month; the Industrial Producer Purchase Price Index (PPIRM) rose even faster at 7.9% year-on-year and 1.1% month-on-month[^2]. The split between industrial inputs and consumer-ready goods widened further: producer prices for means of production jumped 7.4% year-on-year—led by a 27.9% surge in mining and quarrying, an 11.6% rise in raw materials, and a 3.5% increase in processing industries, contributing 5.51 percentage points to overall PPI growth—while factory-gate prices for daily consumer goods edged down 0.1% year-on-year, leaving a 5.0-percentage-point gap between PPI and CPI[^2][^3].

Sector-level breakdowns indicate that imported energy costs, winter coal restocking, and artificial intelligence hardware demand jointly propelled upstream and midstream factory prices. Coal mining and washing prices surged 36.6% year-on-year (+2.7% month-on-month), oil and natural gas extraction climbed 35.0% (+6.0% month-on-month), refined petroleum and nuclear fuel processing rose 29.9%, and chemical raw materials and products increased 16.4%[^2][^3]. At the same time, booming AI computing infrastructure demand and capacity governance supported a 5.9% year-on-year increase in computer, communications, and other electronic equipment manufacturing (+0.1% month-on-month) and a 6.2% rise in electrical machinery and equipment manufacturing (+0.1% month-on-month), while automotive manufacturing prices stabilized month-on-month after three consecutive monthly declines[^2][^3]. NBS methodological notes also specify that starting in January 2026, both CPI and PPI shifted to a 2025 base year, an adjustment that affects monthly year-on-year CPI and PPI readings by an average of roughly 0.06 and 0.08 percentage points respectively; with industrial purchasing costs (+7.9%) outpacing factory-gate selling prices (+5.7%), midstream and downstream manufacturers continue to face margin compression alongside selective hardware price pass-through[^1][^2][^3].
