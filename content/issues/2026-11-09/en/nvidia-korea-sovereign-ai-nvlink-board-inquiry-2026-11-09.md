## From NVLink Fusion Silicon Integration to Dynamo Inference Optimization across South Korea's AI Stack

In announcements published across November 8 and 9, 2026, NVIDIA detailed a comprehensive expansion of its sovereign AI partnership with South Korea's Ministry of Science and ICT (MSIT) and leading Korean technology enterprises, spanning custom silicon integration, data center infrastructure, foundation models, cybersecurity, and workforce training[^1]. At the hardware architecture layer, NVIDIA is supporting Korean neural processing unit (NPU) and XPU developers in exploring integration with the NVIDIA NVLink Fusion ecosystem, enabling domestically designed Korean AI accelerators to interoperate directly with NVIDIA rack-scale systems[^1].

As a flagship deployment of this heterogeneous silicon model, NVIDIA is collaborating with Korean telecommunications carrier SK Telecom and AI chipmaker Rebellions—backed by MSIT—to utilize NVLink Fusion in advancing sovereign AI infrastructure tailored to Korean foundation models and enterprise workloads[^1]. On the software and model layer, under South Korea's national 'AI for All' initiative, NVIDIA is working with three selected consortia and partnering with four leading Korean foundation model developers to optimize and scale AI inference using open-source NVIDIA Dynamo inference software, aimed at maximizing throughput and lowering deployment costs across domestic public and industrial services[^1].

## NVIDIA Board Initiates Independent Committee Review Following Reports of Foreign Intelligence Targeting CEO

Alongside its Asia-Pacific infrastructure announcements, the NVIDIA Board of Directors issued a formal corporate governance statement on November 9, 2026, addressing recent press reports alleging that founder and CEO Jensen Huang had been the target of foreign intelligence efforts[^2]. In response to those reports, the Board announced that it has initiated an independent investigation overseen by a committee composed exclusively of independent directors, assisted by outside independent legal counsel[^2].

The Board's statement carefully delineated the current findings and scope of the review, emphasizing that at present the Board 'has no reason to believe that U.S. national security or NVIDIA trade secrets have been compromised'[^2]. The Board further noted that Jensen Huang fully supports and has committed to cooperating with the review, reaffirmed its confidence in Huang and his executive leadership team, and stated that it will determine whether to share additional information as appropriate once the inquiry progresses[^2]. Taken together, the Korean NVLink Fusion alliance and the board-level counter-intelligence review illustrate how leading AI semiconductor suppliers now operate simultaneously as architects of national computing grids and high-priority subjects of national security governance[^1][^2].
