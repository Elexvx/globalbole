Deutsche Telekom set a target of approximately €2.5 billion in indirect-cost savings from AI and automation by 2030 at its AI Investor Day in Bonn on October 5. The scope is essential: the CFO’s presentation specifies operations outside the US, a 2023 baseline and gross savings before token costs. The amount represents about 15% of indirect costs on that basis, which includes capitalized labour. It is not profit already realized and should not be read as cumulative cash benefits added up year by year from now onward.[^1][^2]

## The nearer-term target for 2027

The nearer checkpoint is 2027. Deutsche Telekom expects AI and automation to generate approximately €1.1 billion in gross financial benefits outside the US, above the roughly €800 million target set at its 2024 Capital Markets Day. The updated estimate includes about €1 billion of indirect-cost savings and €100 million of capital-expenditure savings, excluding token costs. The indirect-cost measure includes capitalized labour, while the capital-expenditure measure excludes it. The company maintains its medium-term financial targets and plans to reinvest part of the gains in digital transformation and German fibre construction, rather than translating all savings directly into additional profit.[^2]

## Customer service and network applications

Among visible applications, the company says its “Frag Magenta” customer-service chatbot handled about 2.6 million calls in the first half of 2026. Its “RAN Guardian Agent” network-management tool anticipates potential network strains and has reduced response times for related events from several hours to about one minute. These are operating metrics disclosed by Deutsche Telekom, not results independently tested for this article. The company also says more than 100,000 employees have received AI training, with people still setting goals, reviewing results and taking responsibility for decisions.[^1]

## New revenue and continuing costs

There are quantified revenue ambitions too. Deutsche Telekom expects approximately €250 million of AI-related revenue from business customers outside the US in 2026, rising to about €800 million by 2030. It sees European enterprises’ demand for control of data and operations as an opportunity, aiming to expand through industrial AI cloud infrastructure, enterprise applications and automation platforms for small and medium-sized businesses. Its enterprise presentation highlights industrial simulation, digital twins and service-process automation.[^1][^3]

Cost discipline will determine how much of those gains remains. The financial presentation aims to keep token costs to no more than a low-double-digit percentage of gross savings, with measures including matching model tiers to tasks, managing vendors and setting token budgets. Large-scale AI adoption is therefore not a one-off tool purchase: continuing inference expense, system integration and organizational change affect how quickly savings targets can become net benefits.[^2]

## From targets to verifiable results

The plan is significant because a traditional telecom operator is assigning explicit financial measures to AI adoption. The meaningful indicators ahead are comparable cost trends, service quality and durable new revenue, rather than the number of tools launched. Deutsche Telekom’s release also cautions that these are forward-looking judgments, with actual results exposed to competition, technological change and other risks. The €2.5 billion figure is consequently a benchmark for future verification, rather than a statement of current performance.[^1]
