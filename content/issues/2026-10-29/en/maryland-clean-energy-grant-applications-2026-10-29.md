Maryland opened Megawatts for Maryland applications on October 26, aiming to increase in-state power supply and ease electricity-price pressures.[^1]

## Allocations for two fiscal years are not proof of payment

The state's Utility RELIEF Act, enacted in 2026, established a statutory framework for the programme, covering competitive support for grid-scale renewable energy and storage projects and providing $100 million for each of fiscal years 2027 and 2028. The two-year total of up to $200 million is a programme-level arrangement, not evidence that the entire amount has been awarded, disbursed or converted into installed capacity.[^2]

For readers, distinguishing allocations from actual results is essential. Applications may proceed to review, and reviews may lead to award decisions; construction, grid connection and operation still follow an award. Collapsing these stages into a claim that investment has already delivered additional electricity obscures what remains to be executed and verified.

## Projects compete over grant requirements, not household retail electricity prices

Eligible projects submit sealed bids quoting subsidy in dollars per MWh-ac of expected grid-delivered electricity over their full commercial lifetimes; Phase 1 closes November 30.[^3]

A low bid is meaningful only if eligibility, feasibility and applicable conditions are satisfied. Ranking bids can help select projects, but it cannot by itself prove that construction will finish on time or guarantee that every project will receive funding. Development costs, actual generation or storage services, and grid-connection arrangements each continue to affect the eventual outcome.

## When can the policy's effects be assessed?

Storage can absorb and release electricity at different times, but its value to the system depends on how it actually operates and connects to the grid. A funding arrangement alone does not establish which constraint it has eased or when. New generation projects likewise have to progress from development arrangements to available supply; budget figures cannot stand in for actual generation data.

The next step is therefore to check award lists, disbursements, construction and grid-connection progress, and operating performance separately, then examine price effects. Timing and transmission still separate project completion from changes in household bills. Until that evidence emerges, the policy cannot be said to have achieved all its objectives.
