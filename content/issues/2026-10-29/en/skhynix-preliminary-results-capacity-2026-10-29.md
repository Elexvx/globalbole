SK hynix reported preliminary third-quarter consolidated K-IFRS revenue of KRW 104.6155 trillion, pending review. Agreements cover over half its total 2027–2028 capacity.[^1]

## Current results and future contracts cannot substitute for one another

Reading this disclosure first requires distinguishing two types of information: results already included in the current accounts and arrangements to be fulfilled in later years. Customers' willingness to make commitments can inform discussion of future demand. Signing a contract, however, does not mean the products have already been manufactured or delivered, still less that the entire contract value has become revenue.

Assessing future supply requires continued attention to product mix, qualified output and actual fulfilment. Contracts alone cannot be used to extrapolate revenue for every quarter. That is also why judging whether an expansion is being realised requires looking beyond financial news to evidence of execution in specific projects.

## A specific expansion project shows why the stages matter

Independent project context helps explain these timing differences. NIST's information on the SK hynix Indiana project describes the West Lafayette facility as a back-end advanced packaging and research-and-development pilot facility for high-bandwidth memory. It lists approximately $3.87 billion in expected corporate capital investment, up to $458 million in federal support, and expected mass production in the second half of 2028.[^2]

This is not a factory newly announced today, nor has this article verified that it has entered mass production. The point is that expansion plans involve specified facility types, investment and expected milestones; a single concept of “adding capacity” cannot replace every stage. NIST's 2024 award announcement also explains that direct funding is disbursed against completed construction, technology, production and commercial milestones. An awarded amount differs from an amount already paid, and listing milestones does not mean they have been achieved.[^3]

## Packaging and validation are practical steps in the delivery chain

Advanced packaging does more than put a shell around a chip. NIST's technical paper explains that packaging provides device protection, communication between chips, power delivery, testing and system integration. Materials, reliable assembly, power density and heat dissipation also constrain one another.[^4]

This provides necessary context for understanding memory capacity: investment in facilities and equipment must pass through the relevant engineering and validation stages before it can yield products customers can use. It does not establish SK hynix's specific yields, defect rates or production costs, and this article has no new data on those measures. Execution at individual company projects still requires corresponding project and shipment evidence.

Subsequent developments should therefore be followed on three separate timelines: completion of financial-data checks, actual fulfilment of customer agreements, and execution of production facilities and technical stages. Progress on any one cannot automatically prove that the other two are complete. Current profits should not simply be extended into a certain outcome for every future quarter.
