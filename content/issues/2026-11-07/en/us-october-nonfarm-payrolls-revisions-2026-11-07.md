## Professional services, health care and warehousing drive October hiring alongside an 84,000 net upward revision to August and September

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) reported on November 6, 2026 (release **USDL-26-1632**) that total nonfarm payroll employment increased by **165,000** in October 2026—well above the prior 12-month average gain of 48,000—while the headline unemployment rate (U-3) edged down by 0.1 percentage point from 4.2% to **4.1%** (representing **7,068,000** unemployed persons). At the same time, August payroll growth was revised up by 53,000 from +133,000 to **+186,000** and September was revised up by 31,000 from +29,000 to **+60,000**, adding a combined **84,000** jobs across the two months and lifting the three-month average payroll gain to 137,000.[^1][^2]

In the establishment survey (CES), total private payrolls expanded by **159,000** in October while government employment rose by 6,000. Job creation was concentrated in three service-providing industries: **professional and business services** added **70,000** jobs (compared with a 15,000 prior 12-month average, led by +33,000 in professional, scientific, and technical services and +18,600 in temporary help services); **private education and health services** rose by **53,000** (with **health care** alone adding **42,000**, including +16,000 in hospitals and +11,000 in home health care); and **transportation and warehousing** gained **24,800** (rounded to **25,000** in the release text, led by +17,000 in couriers and messengers). Manufacturing payrolls increased by **19,000** (+25,000 in durable goods vs. -6,000 in nondurable goods), a monthly change that did not reach statistical significance.[^1][^2]

## Average hourly earnings rise 0.4% to $38.00 while economic part-time employment climbs to 4.84 million

Average hourly earnings for all employees on private nonfarm payrolls rose by **$0.14** (**+0.4%**) over the month to **$38.00** in October and were up **3.1%** over the year; average hourly earnings of private-sector production and nonsupervisory employees increased by $0.12 (+0.4%) to **$32.75**. The average workweek for all private nonfarm employees remained unchanged at **34.4 hours**, with manufacturing weekly hours steady at 40.5 hours (and factory overtime edging up 0.1 hour to 3.1 hours). In the household survey (CPS), the labor force participation rate ticked up 0.1 percentage point to **61.9%** (as the civilian labor force grew by 421,000 to 170.68 million) and the employment-population ratio rose 0.1 percentage point to **59.3%**. However, the number of persons employed **part time for economic reasons** rose by **339,000** in October to **4,840,000**, and the long-term unemployed (jobless for 27 weeks or more) stood at **1,963,000**, accounting for **27.6%** of all unemployed individuals.[^1][^2]

Interpreting the October report requires distinguishing the two underlying surveys and their sampling error thresholds. The establishment survey (CES) samples roughly **119,000** businesses and government agencies covering **622,000** worksites, where the 90-percent confidence interval for a statistically significant monthly payroll change is approximately **±122,000** jobs; the household survey (CPS) samples about **60,000** eligible households, where the 90-percent confidence threshold for monthly employment change is roughly **±650,000**, meaning the 0.1-point move in the unemployment rate to 4.1% is statistically classified as "changed little." Finally, Summary Table A notes that **October 2025 household survey data were not collected due to the federal government shutdown**, leaving a missing year-ago benchmark for household series.[^1][^2]
