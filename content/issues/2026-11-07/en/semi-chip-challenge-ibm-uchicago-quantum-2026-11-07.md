## SEMI’s eight-region semiconductor competition exhibits prize and deadline differences between the press release and official guide

The SEMI Foundation—the nonprofit workforce-development arm of global industry association SEMI, representing more than **4,000** member companies—announced on November 6, 2026, from Milpitas, California, the launch of the inaugural global **Chip the Future Challenge**. Sponsored by **Micron Technology** and Singapore-based **Galatek**, the virtual competition expects **300 to 800** student participants across **eight competing regions** (India, mainland China, Taiwan, Japan, Korea, Southeast Asia, the United States, and Europe), with student teams retaining 100% ownership of their intellectual property and regional winners invited as seed teams to Micron’s global MIMORY Awards. The contest is divided into two tracks: the **Research Challenge** for original semiconductor science, manufacturing, or application research, and the **Innovation Challenge** for engineering solutions creating measurable industry value.[^1][^2]

Cross-checking the November 6 press release against the official *Competition Guide* PDF reveals specific discrepancies in track eligibility, total prize money, and submission dates: whereas the press release states **$10,000** in total prizes ($5,000 first place, $3,000 second, $2,000 third) across undergraduate and graduate teams of 1 to 5 students with abstracts due **November 14, 2026**, and final packages due **January 31, 2027**, the official *Competition Guide* PDF restricts the Research Challenge to **postgraduate students** (teams of **1 to 5** from the same university) and the Innovation Challenge to **undergraduate students** (teams of **2 to 5** from the same university), specifies **$10,000 per track ($20,000 combined)**, and lists a **January 24, 2027** Stage 2 deadline for demos, 8–12 slide decks, and 7-minute pitch videos (following finalist selection on December 1, 2026, for an eight-week mentorship phase ahead of the March 31, 2027 winner announcement).[^1][^2]

## IBM deploys a 120-qubit Nighthawk Quantum System Two in Chicago while WashU joins Anthropic’s AI for Science cohort

Also on November 6, 2026, IBM, the University of Chicago, Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory (Fermilab), and the Illinois Quantum and Microelectronics Park (IQMP) unveiled an **IBM Quantum System Two** installed at UChicago’s Hyde Park Labs. Powered by the **120-qubit IBM Quantum Nighthawk** processor, the system anchors the National Quantum Algorithm Center in Chicago with research partners including the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, Northwestern University, AbbVie, Deloitte, and the Electric Power Research Institute (EPRI), alongside IBM’s FutureNow Chicago center (projected to support 750 jobs). In a companion quantum-classical integration announced November 5–6 with AMD and Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL), IBM paired the 120-qubit Nighthawk processor with ORNL’s **Umbra** cluster (using AMD Instinct MI355X GPUs and 5th Gen EPYC CPUs) to test real-time quantum error correction toward a 2028 target of 100 logical qubits running 100,000 gate operations.[^3]

On the academic AI front, Washington University in St. Louis (WashU) announced in *The Source* on November 6, 2026, that computer science and engineering doctoral candidate **Ziqi Xu**—advised by AI for Health Institute director Professor Chenyang Lu—was selected for **Anthropic’s AI for Science program**. The award provides **six months** of significant Claude API credits to support her project, *"When Should Clinical AI Agents Act? Uncertainty-Aware Delegation for Reliable Decision-Making,"* which studies uncertainty-aware task delegation across specialized clinical models; neither WashU nor Anthropic disclosed the exact dollar value of the six-month API credit grant.[^4]
