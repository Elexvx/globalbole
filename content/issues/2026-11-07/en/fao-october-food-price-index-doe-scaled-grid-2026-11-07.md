## The FAO Food Price Index edges down 1.2% in October while remaining 5.9% above a year ago as 2026 rice output is forecast to contract 2.5%

The Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) reported on November 6, 2026, that the **FAO Food Price Index (FFPI)** averaged **133.9 points** in October 2026, down **1.6 points (-1.2%)** from a revised September reading of 135.6 points, while remaining **7.5 points (+5.9%)** above its October 2025 level and 26.3 points (-16.4%) below its March 2022 peak. Across the five sub-indices, the **Cereal Price Index** fell **4.5%** (-5.5 points) month on month to **116.8 points** (down 12.6% year on year) as wheat retreated after four monthly increases and maize eased from a three-year high in September, though the **FAO All Rice Price Index rose 1.6%** on harvest delays and higher production costs; the **Dairy Price Index** slipped **1.1%** to **117.8 points** (with butter and cheese down 31.1% and 29.3% year on year against a 45.8% jump in skim milk powder); and the **Meat Price Index** edged down **1.2%** to **125.5 points**. Offsetting part of those declines, the **Sugar Price Index** climbed **6.6% (+7.5 points)** for a fourth straight monthly gain to **121.3 points** (**+28.8%** year on year, the highest since December 2024) on rain-disrupted crushing in Brazil’s Centre-South and 2026/27 crop concerns in India, the EU, and Thailand, while the **Vegetable Oil Price Index** rose **0.3%** to **199.2 points** as biofuel demand pushed soybean oil to its highest level since late 2022.[^1]

In its companion *Cereal Supply and Demand Brief* and *Crop Prospects and Food Situation* reports released November 6, FAO forecast 2026 world cereal production at **2,999 million tonnes**—the second-highest on record after 2025—while projecting that global rice production will contract for the first time in four seasons, falling **2.5% to 552.0 million tonnes** amid tight producer margins and El Niño weather. World cereal utilization in 2026/27 is forecast to rise 0.2% to **2,971 million tonnes**, while global cereal stocks by the close of 2027 seasons are projected to expand by 17.2 million tonnes (+1.8%) for a **32.4%** stocks-to-use ratio, reflecting wheat inventory build-ups in the Russian Federation and Ukraine amid Black Sea logistics disruptions. Global cereal trade in 2026/27 is forecast to decline 3.3% to **507.7 million tonnes**, and FAO identified **41 countries and territories** (including 31 in Africa) requiring external food assistance.[^1][^2]

## DOE completes 300,000-barrel Northeast heating oil exchange deliveries and advances its $237 million SCALED grid equipment solicitation

In US energy supply and grid infrastructure, November 6, 2026, marked the scheduled completion deadline for fuel transfers under the Department of Energy’s emergency exchange from the **Northeast Home Heating Oil Reserve (NEHHOR)**. Following Middle East supply disruptions that pushed retail diesel above $6.50 per gallon, DOE awarded contracts on November 4 for **300,000 barrels** of ultra-low sulfur distillate heating oil (**30%** of the 1-million-barrel reserve) stored in Boston, Massachusetts, and New Haven, Connecticut, to Global Companies LLC, Sprague Operating Resources LLC, Trafigura Trading LLC, and Vitol Inc., which must return the volume plus a premium ahead of the **2027–2028 heating season**. While marking only the second NEHHOR release since Hurricane Sandy in 2012, the 300,000-barrel release equals roughly **1.3%** of the 22.551 million barrels in East Coast commercial distillate inventories.[^4]

In parallel, DOE’s Office of Electricity is advancing its **$237 million** *Grid Supply Chain Alternatives to Lead Energy Dominance* (**SCALED**, **DE-FOA-0003641**) Notice of Funding Opportunity: **Topic Area 1 ($50 million)** funds the refurbishment and recertification of retired or storm-damaged power transformers, circuit breakers, and switchgear (3 to 20 awards of $2.5 million to $15 million over up to 24 months), while **Topic Area 2 ($187 million)** scales up domestically sourced alternative materials and subcomponents—including electrical steel, ceramic bushings, copper windings, insulators, and battery energy storage system (BESS) subcomponents—across a 12-month Phase 1 and 36-month Phase 2 (7 to 50 awards of $1 million to $25 million, with a minimum **50% non-federal cost share** across both topics). Concept papers are due **November 20, 2026**, and full applications close **February 8, 2027**. Two caveats apply: FAO’s recent-month Meat Price Index combines observed and projected prices subject to revision, and DOE’s $237 million SCALED solicitation is at the concept-paper stage with award selections anticipated in June 2027.[^1][^3][^4]
