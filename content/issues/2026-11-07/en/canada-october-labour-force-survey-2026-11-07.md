## More job seekers push the unemployment rate to 6.7% as private-sector employment drops by 53,000

Statistics Canada reported in *The Daily* on November 6, 2026, that national employment was little changed in October (**-4,700**, or **0.0%**, standing at 21,100,100, rounded to **21,100,000**) during the October 11–17 reference week, while the employment rate among people aged 15 and older edged down 0.1 percentage point for a third consecutive month to **60.5%** (below the 2017–2019 pre-pandemic average of 62.0%). Because **33,000** more people searched for work (**+2.2%**, lifting total unemployment to **1,507,700**), the national unemployment rate rose 0.2 percentage points to **6.7%**, marking a second straight monthly increase while remaining below the recent peak of 6.9% recorded in April 2026.[^1][^2]

By age group and class of worker, labor market slack was heavily concentrated among young people and private-sector employees. Among **youth aged 15 to 24**, employment dipped by 10,700 (-0.4%)—lowering the youth employment rate to **54.0%**—while the youth unemployment rate jumped 0.7 percentage points to **13.7%** (compared with a 10.8% average over 2017–2019). By contrast, core-aged workers (25 to 54) maintained a steady employment rate of **83.6%** and an unemployment rate of **5.6%** (5.8% for Men+ and 5.3% for Women+), while workers aged 55 and older saw employment edge down by 14,000 and unemployment rise to **5.5%**. Across employer categories, **private-sector employees fell by 53,000** (-0.4%, the first decline since February 2026), largely offset by a **50,000** (+1.1%) rebound in **public-sector employment** following four months of cumulative losses totaling 148,000. By industry, wholesale and retail trade shed **29,000** jobs (-1.0%), whereas construction (**+16,000**) and educational services (**+15,000**) expanded.[^1][^2]

## Alberta and British Columbia diverge as 29.7% of Canadians report household financial difficulty

Provincial outcomes diverged sharply in October: employment in **Alberta** fell by **24,000** (-0.9%), erasing September’s +23,000 gain and pushing Alberta’s unemployment rate up 0.9 percentage points to **7.3%**; **British Columbia** added **12,000** jobs (+0.4%), trimming its unemployment rate to **6.3%**; **Ontario** employment was little changed (-8,600) as its unemployment rate rose 0.3 percentage points to **7.3%** (with the Toronto census metropolitan area reaching 7.4% on an unsmoothed monthly basis); and **Quebec** edged up by 7,500 with unemployment dipping to **5.7%**. On an unadjusted basis, average hourly wages across all employees rose **2.2%** year on year (+$0.83) to **$37.89**, with average usual weekly hours at **35.4 hours**.[^1][^2]

Two supplementary spotlight modules in the October release highlight household budget strain and workplace disparities: **29.7%** of Canadians lived in a household reporting difficulty meeting basic financial needs for transportation, housing, food, and utilities in October 2026—up **2.0 percentage points** from 27.7% in October 2025 and marking the first year-over-year increase since October 2022 (reaching 47.2% among lone-parent families and 38.4%, 36.9%, and 35.9% in the Oshawa, Windsor, and Hamilton metropolitan areas). Meanwhile, **60.9%** of workers aged 15 to 69 reported high job satisfaction (8 to 10 out of 10), with the lowest satisfaction (54.3%) recorded in sales and service occupations, which also had the lowest average hourly wage ($24.19). Methodologically, Statistics Canada’s Labour Force Survey samples roughly **65,000 households** (over 100,000 respondents), evaluates statistical significance at the **68% confidence level**, and excludes the three territories (reported separately as three-month moving averages), persons living on reserves, and full-time Armed Forces members from national totals.[^1][^2]
