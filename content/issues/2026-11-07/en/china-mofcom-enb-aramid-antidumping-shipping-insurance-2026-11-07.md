## Announcements No. 56 and No. 57 target EPDM rubber monomers and para-aramid filament amid 16 EU trade probes in 2026

China’s Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) issued **Announcements No. 56 and No. 57 of 2026** on November 6, 2026, launching 12-month anti-dumping investigations into imported **ethylidene norbornene** (ENB, customs tariff code **29021990**) originating in the **European Union and Japan** and imported **para-aramid filament** (Aramid 1414 filament, tariff codes **54021120, 54026130, and 55011100**) originating in the **European Union**. Both probes cover a dumping investigation period from **July 1, 2025, to June 30, 2026**, and an industrial injury investigation period from **January 1, 2023, to June 30, 2026**, running through November 6, 2027 (extendable by six months) with a 20-day stakeholder registration window. The ENB petition was filed on October 9 by Henghe Material & Science Technology Co., Ltd. for the cyclic olefin monomer used in EPDM rubber for electric-vehicle weatherstripping, power cables, and solar modules; the para-aramid petition was filed on October 12 by Sinochem High Performance Fiber Materials and Tayho Advanced Materials for high-strength fibers used in optical cables, ballistic fabrics, tire cords, and NEV battery enclosures.[^1][^2]

In a companion statement on November 6, the head of MOFCOM’s Trade Remedy and Investigation Bureau noted that preliminary evidence submitted by domestic petitioners showed EU para-aramid filament import volumes remained elevated from 2023 through the first half of 2026 while **cumulative import prices fell by more than 50%**, whereas ENB imports from the EU and Japan **rose 27.35% cumulatively** from 2023 to 2025 and **jumped 56% year on year** in H1 2026 alongside declining prices. The official emphasized that the **European Union has initiated 16 trade remedy investigations against China in 2026**—nearly double the prior year’s pace, including **9 chemical-sector cases (nearly 60%)** and mid-October anti-dumping probes into Chinese furfuryl alcohol and polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF)—while stating that China remains restrained and favors dialogue over protectionism.[^3]

## Seven ministries outline 17 shipping insurance measures covering green vessels, EV exports and 10-day post-hoc conflict filings

Also published on November 6, 2026 (signed September 17, 2026, as document **Jin Fa [2026] No. 16**), the National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA), Ministry of Transport, National Development and Reform Commission, Ministry of Finance, MOFCOM, People’s Bank of China, and SASAC jointly issued the *Guiding Opinions on Accelerating the High-Quality Development of Shipping Insurance*, setting out **17 measures** across six pillars. On coverage design, the blueprint calls for full-lifecycle insurance across oil tankers, LNG carriers, large container ships, **green-fuel and battery-swapping vessels**, and autonomous ship cyber risks; urges full-value underwriting for bulk energy, ore, and grain cargoes; and promotes tailored risk-sharing for electronics, precision instruments, and China’s **"New Three" exports (electric vehicles, lithium batteries, and photovoltaics)**, as well as supply-chain disruption, geopolitical, and polar-route risks.[^4]

On market architecture and supervision, the document backs bilingual Chinese-English standard marine clauses, expanded **renminbi (RMB) settlement** in underwriting, claims, and outbound corporate projects, the establishment of the **Hong Kong Shipowners Mutual Assurance Association** by the China Shipowners Mutual Assurance Association, and a "China Overseas Service Whitelist" of maritime agencies. Notably, Article 12 introduces a fast-track regulatory rule for wartime and geopolitical emergencies: when sudden armed conflicts or geopolitical crises require urgent adjustments to insurance coverage, insurers may use **post-hoc regulatory filing within 10 working days** of the change. Editorially, the two MOFCOM notices represent formal investigation initiations—not preliminary duty impositions—whereas the seven-ministry document establishes a medium-term regulatory and institutional framework.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
