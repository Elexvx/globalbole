On November 11, 2026, [the two-day World Leaders Climate Action Summit officially opened at the UN Climate Change Conference (COP31) in Antalya, Türkiye](https://unfccc.int/cop31), following the launch of the first permanent Electrification Forum co-hosted by the COP31 Presidency and the European Union and backed by an International Energy Agency (IEA) special report on the '35-by-35 Global Electrification Pledge'[^2]. During [a joint press conference by COP31 President Murat Kurum, President of Negotiations Chris Bowen, and UNFCCC Executive Secretary Simon Stiell](https://unfccc.int/news/transcript-of-press-conference-opening-remarks-today-of-cop31-president-president-of-negotiations-un), the Presidency revealed that fuel-importing nations faced roughly $330 billion in additional energy import costs between March and August 2026 alone[^1]. Simultaneously in Washington, [the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) Office of Energy Dominance Financing (EDF) announced the financial close of a $480 million loan for Arena Energy's Project Elkhorn](https://www.energy.gov/articles/energy-department-closes-480-million-loan-expand-american-energy-production-and-strengthen), marking the department's first closed loan supporting domestic oil and natural gas production[^3].

## The 35-by-35 Electrification Pledge: Quadrupling the Annual Pace from 23% Today

According to [the IEA special report commissioned by the joint COP31 Presidency](https://unfccc.int/cop31), electricity currently meets approximately 23% of global final energy consumption—up from 17% in 2005 and 19% in 2015, representing an historical pace of just 0.3 percentage points per year[^1][^2]. The IEA found that implementing 10 priority policy and grid actions could cost-effectively lift electricity's share of final energy consumption to 33% by 2035, within striking distance of the 35% target, while up to 75% of global energy demand can already be electrified using existing technologies[^1][^2]. Australian Minister and COP31 President of Negotiations Chris Bowen emphasized that with artificial intelligence data centers and extreme weather placing unprecedented strain on power grids, reaching 35% electrification by 2035 is essential to keeping the 1.5°C goal alive and requires more than a four-fold increase in the annual global electrification rate[^1].

To institutionalize implementation beyond Antalya, [the COP31 Presidency and the European Union established an ongoing Electrification Board chaired by the Presidency with the IEA acting as secretariat](https://unfccc.int/cop31) and leading a new Global Electrification Tracking Mechanism[^2]. UNFCCC Executive Secretary Simon Stiell noted that according to IRENA, 90% of large-scale renewable projects are now cheaper than fossil fuel alternatives and renewables have officially overtaken coal in global electricity generation, while 180 companies representing nearly $1.7 trillion in combined revenue have called on governments at COP31 to 'seize the electric advantage'[^1]. In a first for UN climate summits, all former COP presidents from COP21 through COP31 also issued a joint declaration endorsing the COP31 Action Agenda targets[^1].

## DOE Closes First $480M Offshore Hydrocarbon Loan Under Energy Dominance Financing

In sharp policy contrast to the multilateral electrification push in Antalya, [the U.S. Department of Energy confirmed on November 10 that its Office of Energy Dominance Financing closed a $480 million loan to Arena Energy for Project Elkhorn](https://www.energy.gov/articles/energy-department-closes-480-million-loan-expand-american-energy-production-and-strengthen), financing the modernization of 22 offshore production platforms off the coast of Louisiana[^3]. Arena Energy currently produces roughly 30,000 barrels of oil per day offshore; at peak capacity, Project Elkhorn is projected to add up to 38,600 barrels of oil per day and 77 million cubic feet of natural gas per day, totaling approximately 125 million barrels of oil and 250 billion cubic feet of natural gas over the project's lifespan while supporting 2,400 construction jobs and 400 operations roles[^3].

U.S. Secretary of Energy Chris Wright and EDF Director Gregory A. Beard stated that the financing—enabled by the Working Families Tax Cut—puts existing offshore corridors back to work while generating returns for taxpayers[^3]. Taken together, the Antalya 35-by-35 electrification framework and the DOE's $480 million offshore loan underscore the diverging capital allocation strategies shaping global energy markets in late 2026: energy-importing economies are accelerating end-use electrification across transport, buildings, and industry to insulate budgets from $330 billion fossil price shocks, while major hydrocarbon producers deploy federal credit facilities to extend output from mature offshore basins[^1][^2][^3].
