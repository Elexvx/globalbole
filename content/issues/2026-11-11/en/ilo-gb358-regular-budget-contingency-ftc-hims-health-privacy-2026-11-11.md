Between November 10 and November 11, 2026, international labor governance and digital consumer protection authorities issued two major compliance actions: in Geneva, [the 358th Session of the International Labour Organization (ILO) Governing Body adopted its formal decision on the regular budget financial position and contingency measures (GB.358/PFA/4(Rev.1))](https://www.ilo.org/resource/record-decisions/gb/gb358/decision-concerning-update-financial-position-regular-budget-and), recommending that the Office continue efforts to avert or minimize involuntary terminations through non-staff savings alongside a companion [decision on the implementation of the ILO reform process (GB.358/INS/7)](https://www.ilo.org/resource/record-decisions/gb/gb358/decision-concerning-update-implementation-ilo-reform)[^1][^2]. On the same day in Washington and San Francisco, [the U.S. Federal Trade Commission (FTC) voted 2–0 to file a First Amended Complaint adding five states—Arizona, Florida, Nebraska, Ohio, and Wisconsin—to its federal lawsuit against telehealth provider Hims & Hers](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/11/five-states-join-ftc-lawsuit-alleging-hims-hers-engaged-deceptive-unlawful-privacy-practices) over alleged unlawful health data sharing and deceptive subscription billing[^3].

## ILO 358th Governing Body Decisions: Prioritizing Non-Staff Savings and Voluntary Measures Through March 2027

According to [the official record of decisions from the Programme, Financial and Administrative Section of the 358th Session](https://www.ilo.org/resource/record-decisions/gb/gb358/decision-concerning-update-financial-position-regular-budget-and), the Governing Body reviewed the updated financial situation and savings outlook for the regular budget following the contingency package adopted at its 357th bis (Special) Session[^1]. Taking note of the non-staff savings already achieved, voluntary staff measures, and the ongoing workforce adjustment process, the tripartite Governing Body explicitly recommended that the International Labour Office continue its efforts to avert or minimize involuntary staff terminations by relying on non-staff administrative savings, and requested a comprehensive progress report at its 359th Session in March 2027[^1].

In a parallel institutional governance step, [the Governing Body adopted Decision GB.358/INS/7 on the implementation of the broader ILO reform](https://www.ilo.org/resource/record-decisions/gb/gb358/decision-concerning-update-implementation-ilo-reform), instructing the Director-General to incorporate tripartite guidance from the November deliberations and present an updated implementation report at the 359th Session in March 2027[^2]. By formally prioritizing non-staff cost reductions and voluntary transition pathways over compulsory layoffs during a period of multilateral budget strain, the UN labor agency's governing council set an institutional benchmark for public-sector workforce restructuring[^1][^2].

## FTC Expands Federal Telehealth Lawsuit to Seven State Co-Plaintiffs Over Ad Tracking and Subscription Dark Patterns

In digital healthcare and consumer subscription markets, [the FTC filed its First Amended Complaint in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California on November 10](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/11/five-states-join-ftc-lawsuit-alleging-hims-hers-engaged-deceptive-unlawful-privacy-practices), expanding the coalition of state co-plaintiffs to seven by adding Arizona, Florida, Nebraska, Ohio, and Wisconsin alongside initial partners California (via Los Angeles County Counsel) and Utah[^3]. Initially filed in July 2026, the lawsuit alleges that Hims & Hers deceived patients by promising to keep their sensitive medical and health intake information private while sharing that data with third-party advertising platforms including Meta and Snap[^3].

Beyond health privacy violations, [the amended complaint alleges that Hims & Hers failed to clearly disclose that consumers are charged for prescriptions almost immediately after submitting an online intake form—despite marketing claims that users would first consult a medical provider to determine whether a treatment is 'right for them'—and erected obstructive barriers against subscription cancellation](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/11/five-states-join-ftc-lawsuit-alleging-hims-hers-engaged-deceptive-unlawful-privacy-practices)[^3]. Charging violations of the FTC Act, the Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act (ROSCA), and the consumer protection statutes of all seven participating states, the enforcement action underscores heightened regulatory scrutiny over telehealth billing practices and ad-tech data leakage heading into 2027[^1][^2][^3].
