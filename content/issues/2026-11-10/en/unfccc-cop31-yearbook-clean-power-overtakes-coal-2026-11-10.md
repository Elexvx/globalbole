## Ten Years of the Marrakech Partnership: Progress on 44 of 96 Global Indicators and 84% of Acceleration Plans

As the 31st UN Climate Change Conference (COP31, November 9–20, 2026) in Antalya, Türkiye, advanced into its thematic implementation sessions, the UN Climate Change secretariat (UNFCCC) published the 2026 Yearbook of Global Climate Action.[^1] Marking the tenth anniversary of the Marrakech Partnership for Global Climate Action, the report evaluates 96 global socio-economic indicators aligned with the Paris Agreement’s first global stocktake across the six thematic priorities of the COP31 Action Agenda, confirming measurable progress across 44 global indicators alongside expanding renewable energy capacity and slowing deforestation.[^1]

UN Climate Change Executive Secretary Simon Stiell emphasized that the 2026 Yearbook demonstrates how businesses, cities, and subnational regions are translating climate pledges into tangible economic outcomes, while urging COP31 delegates to mobilize finance and accelerate real-economy delivery.[^1] Drawing on a record volume of submissions to the Non-State Actor Zone for Climate Action (NAZCA) portal, the Yearbook documents more than 200 concrete actions reported by 83 Cooperative Climate Initiatives (CCIs) and shows that 84% of multi-stakeholder Plans to Accelerate Solutions (PAS) reported progress after one year of implementation—while cautioning that self-reported non-state disclosures do not replace binding national inventories and that major gaps remain in oil and gas methane emissions and food loss.[^1]

## Singapore Launches Fifth COP Pavilion in Antalya With Over 60 Programs Across Carbon Markets and Decarbonization

Reinforcing COP31’s focus on practical execution, the Government of Singapore officially inaugurated the Singapore Pavilion in Antalya on November 10, 2026, under the theme 'Shared Resilience, Strengthened by Community', marking Singapore’s fifth consecutive year hosting a national pavilion at COP.[^2] Led by Minister for Sustainability and the Environment and Minister-in-charge of Trade Relations Grace Fu, the launch ceremony featured COP31 President of Negotiations and Australian Climate Minister Chris Bowen, UK Special Representative for Climate Rachel Kyte, International Maritime Organization (IMO) Secretary-General Arsenio Dominguez, FinDev Canada CEO Lori Kerr, and Singapore’s Ambassador for Climate Action Ravi Menon.[^2]

According to Singapore’s Ministry of Sustainability and the Environment (MSE), the pavilion will convene more than 60 events over the two weeks of COP31 spanning seven priority tracks: Adaptation and Resilience, Carbon Markets, Cities and Zero Waste, Energy and Decarbonisation, Finance, Transport, and Youth, including four sessions organized by 20 delegates from the Climate Youth Development Programme (CYDP).[^2] Together, the UNFCCC’s 96-indicator stocktake scorecard and the cross-border carbon finance and maritime decarbonization dialogues in Antalya highlight how COP31 is shifting emphasis from headline pledges toward auditable sectoral implementation.[^1][^2]
