## Geneva Tripartite Mobilization: ILO Launches 2026–2029 Decent Work and Recovery Programme for MSMEs and Workers

Following the mandate of the 355th Session of the International Labour Organization (ILO) Governing Body, ILO Director-General Gilbert F. Houngbo convened a high-level Partners’ Meeting at ILO Headquarters in Geneva on November 10, 2026 (13:30–15:00 CET), co-hosted with Qatar’s Minister of Labour Ali bin Saeed bin Samikh Al Marri and the Netherlands’ Permanent Representative Erica Schouten.[^1] The session brought together Palestinian Minister of Labour Enas Dahadha Attari, Palestinian General Federation of Trade Unions (PGFTU) Secretary General Shaher Saed, Federation of Palestinian Chambers of Commerce, Industry and Agriculture (FPCCIA) President Abdu Idris, UNSCO Deputy Special Coordinator Ramiz Alakbarov, and ILO Regional Director for Arab States Ruba Jaradat.[^1]

The Geneva meeting mobilized technical and financial commitments for the new Decent Work and Recovery Programme (DWRP) 2026–2029, the strategic framework guiding ILO operations across the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, and the Gaza Strip.[^1] Addressing severe contractions in women’s and youth labor force participation, widespread asset losses among micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises (MSMEs), and weakened occupational safety and health (OSH) oversight, the DWRP places employment-intensive reconstruction, enterprise recovery, social protection, and tripartite dialogue at the center of early recovery—while noting that operational outcomes depend on sustained donor financing and ground access.[^1]

## OECD 32-Country Study Finds Multigenerational Teams Offset Ageing Headwinds as Green Transition Models Show Net Job Gains

On the same day, the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) released a 75-page working paper titled 'Workforce ageing and productivity: An analysis by industry characteristics' (authored by Vassiliki Koutsogeorgopoulou and Jen-Hsuan Yu), examining industry-level panel data across 32 OECD economies from 2008 to 2023.[^2] Challenging uniform demographic pessimism, the empirical study finds that workforce ageing does not exert a direct negative first-order drag on industry productivity on average, though substantial heterogeneity emerges across different industrial structures.[^2]

Specifically, while an older workforce is associated with lower productivity in ICT-intensive industries and sectors experiencing skill mismatches, accumulated worker experience correlates with higher productivity in high-labor-demand industries that maintain strong age diversity—underscoring how multigenerational teams, lifelong learning, and older-worker job mobility can offset demographic headwinds.[^2] Complementing this labor research, three macroeconomic modeling papers released by the OECD on November 10 project that—contingent on large-scale investment mobilization—accelerated net-zero energy transitions could raise GDP relative to baseline policies by roughly 1.5% in South Africa by 2035, 1.23% in Viet Nam by 2045 (alongside carbon pricing revenues of 0.65% of GDP), and 1.4% in Indonesia by 2040 with a net gain of around 600,000 jobs.[^2]
