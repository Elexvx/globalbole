## Resolution 76/262 Mandate Triggered: 81st UN General Assembly Debates Security Council Veto at 10,260th Meeting

On November 10, 2026, the 81st Session of the United Nations General Assembly convened a formal plenary meeting in the General Assembly Hall in New York under agenda item 62, 'Use of the veto'.[^1] Convened by the President of the 81st General Assembly, H.E. Dr. Khalilur Rahman, the session was triggered automatically under the standing mandate established by General Assembly Resolution 76/262.[^1]

According to the formal notification letter addressed to all Permanent Representatives and Permanent Observers, the plenary debate followed a veto cast by a permanent member of the Security Council at its 10,260th meeting on October 30, 2026, under the agenda item concerning the situation in Western Sahara.[^1] Adopted in 2022, Resolution 76/262 obliges the General Assembly President to convene a formal plenary meeting within ten working days whenever one or more permanent members cast a veto; while the General Assembly cannot legally overturn a Security Council veto, the November 10 debate compels permanent members to justify their votes before all 193 Member States and places broad multilateral scrutiny on the official UN record.[^1]

## From Crisis Response to Prospective Planning: WMO Europe, UNCCD, and EU DG Environment Convene Drought Governance Forum

While New York focused on multilateral security accountability, European meteorological and hydrological authorities convened on November 10, 2026 (09:00–12:40 CET), for a regional policy and technical forum organized by the World Meteorological Organization (WMO) Regional Association VI (Europe) High-Level Task Team for Early Warning for All (HLTT EW4All) alongside the UN Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD), titled 'Proactive Drought Management – How to achieve it?'.[^2] Opening sessions were led by Sari Lappi of the WMO Regional Office for Europe and Daniel Tsegai of the UNCCD.[^2]

During the governance segment, Lukas Repa of the European Commission’s Directorate-General for Environment (DG Environment) presented core operational takeaways from the EU’s new Common Implementation Strategy (CIS) Technical Guidance on Drought Management Plans, complemented by socio-hydrological research from the University of Amsterdam and the Global Water Partnership aimed at avoiding maladaptation in water infrastructure.[^2] Experts from the Danish Meteorological Institute, Czechia’s T. G. Masaryk Water Research Institute, Spain’s Guadalquivir River Basin Authority, the Netherlands’ Rijkswaterstaat, and the Israel Water Authority examined national implementation frameworks that link hydrometeorological early warnings directly to pre-agreed basin allocation rules ahead of acute drought emergencies.[^2]
