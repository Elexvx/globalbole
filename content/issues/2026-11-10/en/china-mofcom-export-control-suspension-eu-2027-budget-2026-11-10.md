## Eighth-Round Trade Consensus: Two-Month Extension of Announcement No. 21 Export Control Suspension for 16 US Entities

In an official policy statement released on November 10, 2026, a spokesperson for the Ministry of Commerce of China (MOFCOM) announced that to implement the consensus arrangements reached during the eighth round of China-US economic and trade consultations, China has decided to continue suspending the export control measures under MOFCOM Announcement No. 21 of 2025 for two months starting November 10, 2026.[^1] Originally issued on April 4, 2025, Announcement No. 21 placed 16 US entities on China’s export control list and prohibited the export of dual-use items to those entities.[^1] Extending this non-tariff pause for two months aligns directly with the parallel two-month extension of China’s 24% additional tariff suspension on US-origin imports taking effect the same day, creating a synchronized bilateral trade buffer.[^1]

The spokesperson detailed the statutory licensing pathway that applies during the two-month suspension window: exporters seeking to supply dual-use items to the 16 listed US entities must submit formal applications to the Ministry of Commerce in accordance with the Regulations of the People’s Republic of China on the Export Control of Dual-Use Items.[^1] MOFCOM stated that it will conduct case-by-case statutory reviews and grant export licenses to applications that satisfy end-user and end-use compliance requirements, preserving the legal structure and reversibility of the export control list while restoring a predictable compliance channel for cross-border industrial and technology supply chains through early January 2027.[^1]

## EU Economic and Financial Affairs Council Enters Conciliation Committee Over the 2027 Union Budget

As Beijing and Washington activated their two-month tariff and export-control window, European fiscal policymakers gathered in Brussels on November 10, 2026, for the decisive stage of the 2027 European Union annual budget process. According to the official media advisory issued by the Council of the European Union, the Economic and Financial Affairs Council (Budget) convened at 09:00 CET in the Europa building under the chairmanship of Simon Harris, Tánaiste and Minister for Finance of Ireland, acting in his capacity as chief Council negotiator for the 2027 EU budget.[^2]

Following a public roundtable session beginning around 09:05 focused on the Union budget for 2027, Council negotiators entered the formal Conciliation Committee meeting with representatives of the European Parliament at approximately 10:45 to reconcile commitment and payment appropriations within the statutory timeframe.[^2] With member states balancing heightened defense readiness, clean-tech competitiveness investments, and tight ceilings under the EU’s multiannual financial framework, both the November 10 Brussels budget conciliation and the US-China non-tariff licensing arrangement underscore concerted efforts by major economies to contain near-term supply-chain and fiscal uncertainty heading into 2027.[^1][^2]
