Seasonally adjusted U.S. wholesale prices rose 0.3 percent in October and climbed 6.5 percent on an unadjusted 12-month basis — the steepest annual increase since November 2022 — according to the [October 2026 Producer Price Index (PPI) news release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/ppi_11132026.htm) published by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) on November 13[^1]. On the same day, the U.S. Census Bureau issued [Tip Sheet Number 23 (TP26-23)](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/tip-sheets/2026/tp26-23.html), detailing newly published household pulse data covering employment and energy expenses across 136,000 households alongside upcoming regional workforce and business payroll releases[^2].

## Truck Freight and Organic Chemicals Drive October PPI as Intermediate Goods Climb 14.6% Year-on-Year

Following monthly final demand advances of 1.4 percent in September and 0.4 percent in August, October's 0.3 percent gain reflected persistent core pipeline pressures. According to [BLS's detailed FD-ID tables](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/ppi_11132026.htm), final demand less foods, energy, and trade services rose 0.5 percent in October — matching September's pace — and advanced 4.9 percent over the 12 months ended October[^1]. Final demand goods rose 0.5 percent on the month and 10.2 percent year-on-year (the largest 12-month rise since October 2022), propelled by basic organic chemicals (+3.9 percent) and foods (+1.3 percent), even as diesel fuel retreated 7.9 percent on the month[^1].

Final demand services edged up 0.2 percent for a fifth consecutive monthly increase (+5.0 percent year-on-year), with more than 80 percent of October's advance driven by a 3.9 percent monthly jump in truck transportation of freight (+24.6 percent over 12 months), lifting overall transportation and warehousing services by 2.0 percent[^1]. Within labor-linked business services, staffing services stood 3.9 percent higher year-on-year while executive search fees declined 4.6 percent in October; upstream, processed goods for intermediate demand rose 14.6 percent year-on-year and intermediate services climbed 6.8 percent amid a 9.2 percent jump in metals, minerals, and ores wholesaling margins, as BLS incorporated sample updates across nine NAICS industries[^1].

## Census Bureau Publishes 136,000-Household Pulse Survey and 23-Year Commuting Employment Series

Supply-chain and utility cost shifts are being tracked directly at the household and establishment levels across federal statistical programs. As documented in the [Census Bureau's November 13 tip sheet](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/tip-sheets/2026/tp26-23.html), the agency released new Household Trends and Outlook Pulse Survey (HTOPS) tables and public-use microdata on November 12, capturing responses from roughly 136,000 households surveyed between September 9 and October 12 on employment status, residential energy expenses, mental health, and adult social connectedness[^2].

For regional labor market planners, the Census Bureau also updated its Longitudinal Employer-Household Dynamics (LEHD) Origin-Destination Employment Statistics (LODES) and OnTheMap application with 2024 tabulations — creating a continuous 23-year commuting and employment dataset from 2002 to 2024 — ahead of a November 18 Quarterly Workforce Indicators (QWI) regional workforce briefing[^2]. Upcoming releases on the calendar include the November 17 Current Population Survey supplement on America's Families and Living Arrangements, biweekly Business Trends and Outlook Survey (BTOS) readings across the 25 largest metro areas, the December 3 Annual Business Survey (ABS) and Nonemployer Statistics by Demographics (NES-D) on firm payrolls, and the December 10 Small Area Income and Poverty Estimates (SAIPE) spanning 3,143 counties and 13,137 school districts[^2].

## Sticky Freight Distribution Margins Meet Granular Household Living-Cost Benchmarks

Read together, the October PPI release and the Census Bureau's high-frequency survey calendar illustrate why businesses and households continue to experience uneven cost pressures despite a monthly pullback in crude petroleum (-10.3 percent) and diesel prices[^1]. Elevated freight trucking rates, industrial metals wholesaling margins, and intermediate manufacturing input costs remain embedded in domestic distribution pipelines heading into year-end[^1].

By pairing monthly producer price indices with HTOPS household energy-bill tracking, biweekly BTOS employer sentiment, and county-level income and payroll benchmarks, U.S. statistical agencies are equipping policymakers and employers with sharper visibility into real-economy cost transmission and labor market resilience[^1][^2].
