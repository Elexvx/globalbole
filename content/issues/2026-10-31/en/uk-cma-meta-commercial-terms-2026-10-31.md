The UK Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) announced on October 30 that it was investigating Meta’s terms for business users under the Competition Act 1998. The regulator is concerned that the terms could allow Meta to collect content from the websites of businesses and news publishers using its services and use it to develop and provide its own products, including AI features, without those businesses having a choice over how their content is used. The central issue is not simply AI access to web content: it also concerns whether a platform has sufficient market power to impose unfair trading conditions on businesses that rely on it to reach audiences. The case page says the investigation concerns existing terms or amendments to them, not only changes that have yet to take effect.[^1][^2]

## A paused rollout, not a closed investigation

The timeline matters. According to the CMA, Meta initially intended to change its terms on October 30, then moved implementation to November 20 following the regulator’s intervention. On October 28, Meta announced that it would pause the rollout pending consultation with industry bodies. **Pausing the rollout does not mean the regulator has approved the terms or that the investigation has ended.** The CMA is examining whether Meta holds a dominant position in relevant UK markets and, if so, whether its terms constitute an abuse of that position. The regulator stressed that the investigation is at an early stage and that it has not found Meta to have infringed competition law.[^1]

Machine-readable signals through which websites express AI-training preferences address a related but different issue. Such signals help websites communicate their choices; the CMA case examines commercial terms and market power. A `robots.txt` configuration cannot substitute for a competition-law assessment. The CMA is separately seeking information on Meta’s business subscription services and external links, but says these matters **fall outside the scope of this formal investigation**.[^1]

## The investigation’s next steps

The case page’s initial timetable provides for rapid information gathering and engagement with relevant parties between October 2026 and January 2027. This is an indicative schedule subject to change, not a deadline for a ruling. The investigation could close without further action. If the CMA issues preliminary allegations, the businesses concerned would still have an opportunity to respond before a final decision could follow. Opening an investigation therefore must not be reported as the imposition of a fine or a ban.[^2]
