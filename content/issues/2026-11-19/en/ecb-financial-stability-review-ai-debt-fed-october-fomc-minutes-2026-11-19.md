On November 18, 2026, the [European Central Bank (ECB)](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.pr261118~83c5c60158.en.html) in Frankfurt published its November 2026 *Financial Stability Review*, hours before the [Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261118a.htm) in Washington released the minutes of the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) meeting held on October 27–28, 2026. Striking a remarkably unified tone across the Atlantic, both central banks warned that heavily debt-financed artificial intelligence infrastructure, stretched equity valuations, and sovereign fiscal imbalances have heightened the risk of abrupt cross-asset market repricing[^1][^2].

## ECB Flags Debt-Funded AI Infrastructure and Leveraged Sovereign Bondholders

Presenting the [November 2026 Financial Stability Review](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.pr261118~83c5c60158.en.html), ECB Vice-President Boris Vujčić emphasized that while euro-area banks maintain solid capital ratios and profitability, global markets remain highly vulnerable to sudden sentiment shifts. The ECB highlighted that equity market gains are narrowly concentrated in a cluster of interconnected AI firms priced for ambitious earnings growth, while the massive build-out of AI compute infrastructure is increasingly funded through debt markets rather than internal cash flows. Any disappointment in AI monetization could trigger synchronized corrections across equity and corporate debt markets, amplified by leverage within non-bank financial intermediaries (NBFIs)[^1].

On the fiscal and credit front, the [ECB assessment](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.pr261118~83c5c60158.en.html) cautioned that persistent budget deficits in several advanced economies are colliding with a structural shift in bond ownership toward news-sensitive foreign investors and leveraged investment funds, raising the specter of sudden term-premium spikes. At the same time, the lagged balance-sheet impact of trade tariffs continues to weigh on trade-exposed euro-area manufacturers. To bolster European capital resilience, the ECB called for rapid progress on the EU Savings and Investments Union while ensuring regulatory simplification does not erode international prudential standards[^1].

## Federal Reserve Minutes Detail Circular AI Financing, Private Credit Stress, and Rate Hike Projections

The [Federal Reserve's October 27–28 FOMC minutes](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261118a.htm) corroborated the ECB's warnings with granular financial stability findings. Federal Reserve policymakers and staff flagged three structural vulnerabilities in the AI boom: substantial off-balance-sheet data-center leasing commitments that obscure true corporate leverage, circular financing arrangements among chipmakers, cloud providers, and AI startups that artificially inflate revenue metrics, and a recent widening of high-yield bond spreads for data-center construction projects. Furthermore, the minutes noted a sharp second-quarter rise in the share of distressed software loans within private credit portfolios as investors reassessed the disruptive threat of generative AI to legacy software borrowers[^2].

Regarding macroeconomic conditions and monetary policy, the [FOMC minutes](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261118a.htm) recorded U.S. headline Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) inflation at 3.6 percent in September (with core PCE at 3.1 percent), an unemployment rate of 4.2 percent, and three-month average payroll gains slowing to roughly 50,000 per month. Median responses in the Open Market Desk survey projected 25-basis-point rate increases in December 2026 and March 2027, pushed the expected longer-run nominal federal funds rate to 3.4 percent, and reduced reserve management purchases (RMPs) to zero. The minutes also explicitly highlighted European sovereign stress, noting that the 10-year French-German sovereign yield spread surged during the intermeeting period to levels unseen since the 2011 euro-area debt crisis[^2].
