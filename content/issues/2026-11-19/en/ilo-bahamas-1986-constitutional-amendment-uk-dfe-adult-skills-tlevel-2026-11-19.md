On November 18, 2026, the [International Labour Organization (ILO)](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/bahamas-ratifies-1986-instrument-amendment-ilo-constitution) in Geneva announced that the Government of The Bahamas has deposited its instrument of ratification for the 1986 Instrument of Amendment to the ILO Constitution, becoming the 132nd Member State to endorse the governance reform. On the same day, the [UK Department for Education (DfE)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/dfe-update-18-november-2026/dfe-update-local-authorities-18-november-2026) issued its November 18 operational directive to local authorities and further education providers, opening the December 2026 Adult Skills Fund (ASF) performance-management review and establishing binding submission deadlines for 16-to-19 T-Level technical qualification data[^1][^2].

## The Bahamas Ratifies 1986 ILO Constitutional Amendment as Entry Into Force Awaits Three Chief Industrial States

According to the [ILO announcement](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/bahamas-ratifies-1986-instrument-amendment-ilo-constitution), the 1986 Constitutional Amendment was adopted to make the composition of the ILO Governing Body more representative of all geographic regions by abolishing permanent non-elective seats, while also modernizing the procedure for appointing the ILO Director-General and the rules governing future constitutional amendments. Under Article 36 of the existing ILO Constitution, an amendment requires ratification or acceptance by two-thirds of all ILO Member States, including at least five of the ten Members of chief industrial importance[^1].

With the registration of The Bahamas' ratification on November 16, 2026, the [ILO tally](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/bahamas-ratifies-1986-instrument-amendment-ilo-constitution) reached 132 out of 187 Member States, including India and Italy among the states of chief industrial importance. To bring the landmark governance reform into force, the organization now requires ratifications from at least three of the remaining eight Members of chief industrial importance—Brazil, China, France, Germany, Japan, the Russian Federation, the United Kingdom, or the United States[^1].

## UK Department for Education Opens December Adult Skills Fund Review and Enforces T-Level Funding Deadlines

At the national workforce-training level, the [UK Department for Education's November 18 update](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/dfe-update-18-november-2026/dfe-update-local-authorities-18-november-2026) set an immediate compliance calendar for vocational and adult education funding. The DfE opened the December 2026 performance-management review for the 2026-to-2027 Adult Skills Fund, giving grant-funded and contract-funded providers until 5:00 p.m. on December 2, 2026, to submit digital requests for Department for Work and Pensions (DWP)-backed Free Courses for Jobs (FCFJ) allocations or top-ups of up to GBP 45,000, alongside exceptional growth and exemption requests. Final reconciliation statements for the 2025-to-2026 academic year covering the Adult Skills Fund, Free Courses for Jobs, and the Advanced Learner Loans Bursary (ALLB) will be issued by the end of November, with payments and recoveries settled in December 2026[^2].

To safeguard funding accuracy for youth technical education, the [DfE notice](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/dfe-update-18-november-2026/dfe-update-local-authorities-18-november-2026) instructed schools and colleges delivering 16-to-19 T-Level programmes to verify their student records before two strict cutoffs: schools submitting via the autumn school census must request any T-Level data amendments by November 25, 2026, while colleges submitting Individualised Learner Record (ILR) data must finalize accurate R04 returns by December 4, 2026. The department also clarified that following the September 2026 expansion of Free School Meals (FSM) to all households receiving Universal Credit, the Holiday Activities and Food (HAF) programme will retain the targeted GBP 7,400 earned-income threshold so holiday nutrition and childcare resources remain focused on the lowest-income families[^2].
