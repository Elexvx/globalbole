On November 18, 2026, the [World Health Organization (WHO)](https://www.who.int/news/item/18-11-2026-new-who-collaborating-centre-established-to-support-work-on-foodborne-disease-burden) formally designated the Service Health Information within the Epidemiology and Public Health Directorate at Sciensano—Belgium's national scientific institute in Brussels uniting human, animal, and environmental health—as the new WHO Collaborating Centre on Foodborne Disease Burden. On the same day, the [European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (ECDC)](https://www.ecdc.europa.eu/en/publications-data/rapid-risk-assessment-carbapenem-resistant-enterobacterales-fourth-update) in Stockholm published its fourth Rapid Risk Assessment on carbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales (CRE), while the [European Medicines Agency (EMA)](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/events/clinical-trials-information-system-ctis-bitesize-talk-ctis-training-environment-role-management) in Amsterdam rolled out upgraded safety role management and sandbox onboarding for the European Union's Clinical Trials Information System (CTIS)[^1][^2][^3].

## WHO Designates Belgium's Sciensano to Advance Global Foodborne Disease Statistical Modeling

According to the [WHO announcement](https://www.who.int/news/item/18-11-2026-new-who-collaborating-centre-established-to-support-work-on-foodborne-disease-burden), Sciensano's designation builds on years of close collaboration with the WHO Foodborne Disease Burden Epidemiology Reference Group (FERG). Under its new mandate, the Brussels centre will develop and compute statistical models to estimate the global, regional, and national burden of foodborne illnesses, collect and synthesize epidemiological mortality and morbidity data, assist the WHO in tracking global food safety indicators, and provide hands-on capacity building for Member States conducting national burden-of-disease studies within a One Health framework[^1].

While statistical modeling strengthens preventative food safety, European hospitals are confronting an accelerating crisis in antimicrobial resistance. In its [fourth CRE Rapid Risk Assessment update](https://www.ecdc.europa.eu/en/publications-data/rapid-risk-assessment-carbapenem-resistant-enterobacterales-fourth-update), drawing on the European Antimicrobial Resistance Genes Surveillance Network (EURGen-Net) and the 2025 CRE survey (CRE25), the ECDC reported a steady epidemiological deterioration across the EU and European Economic Area since its previous assessment in 2023. Most EU/EEA countries recorded a sustained rise in bloodstream infections caused by carbapenem-resistant *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, driving higher clinical mortality and prolonged intensive-care stays[^2].

## ECDC Flags Rising Metallo-Beta-Lactamase Superbugs While EMA Refines Clinical Trial Safety Roles

Crucially, the [ECDC genomic evaluation](https://www.ecdc.europa.eu/en/publications-data/rapid-risk-assessment-carbapenem-resistant-enterobacterales-fourth-update) identified two converging biological threats. First, an increasing proportion of *Klebsiella pneumoniae* isolates now carry New Delhi metallo-beta-lactamase (NDM) carbapenemases, which render several newer beta-lactam/beta-lactamase inhibitor drug combinations approved since 2016 clinically ineffective. Second, surveillance detected a higher prevalence of hypervirulence markers—specifically yersiniabactin and aerobactin—alongside the rapid cross-border spread of *Escherichia coli* strains carrying the `blaOXA-244` gene. The agency urged national health authorities to enforce stringent infection prevention and control protocols, whole-genome sequencing surveillance, and antimicrobial stewardship[^2].

To support the safe clinical development of next-generation therapeutics across Europe, the [European Medicines Agency's November 18 CTIS session](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/events/clinical-trials-information-system-ctis-bitesize-talk-ctis-training-environment-role-management) detailed major operational refinements under the EU Clinical Trials Regulation (Regulation (EU) No 536/2014). The update introduces dedicated Safety user roles within the CTIS Safety Module to ensure granular, least-privilege access control over pharmacovigilance and adverse-event submissions, alongside a revamped CTIS Training Environment access workflow administered through Test EMA Account Management for commercial and academic trial sponsors[^3].
