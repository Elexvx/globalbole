On November 13, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved [neoadjuvant durvalumab (Imfinzi) combined with enfortumab vedotin-ejfv followed by adjuvant single-agent durvalumab after radical cystectomy](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-durvalumab-enfortumab-vedotin-ejfv-muscle-invasive-bladder-cancer) for adult patients with muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) who are ineligible for or refuse cisplatin-based chemotherapy, acting six weeks ahead of its regulatory goal date[^1]. On the same day at World Health Organization (WHO) headquarters in Geneva, more than 400 young leaders from over 50 countries closed the fourth Global Model WHO by adopting the [Youth Declaration on Plastics and Health](https://www.who.int/news/item/13-11-2026-put-health-in-the-treaty---youth-delegates-at-the-global-model-who-2026-adopt-declaration-on-plastics-and-health), urging negotiators to embed binding health protections into the global plastics treaty[^2].

## Phase 3 VOLGA Trial Cuts Progression and Mortality Risks in Cisplatin-Ineligible MIBC

According to the [FDA Oncology Approval Notification](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-durvalumab-enfortumab-vedotin-ejfv-muscle-invasive-bladder-cancer), clinical efficacy was established in VOLGA (NCT04960709), a randomized (1:1:1), open-label Phase 3 trial enrolling 695 MIBC patients scheduled for radical cystectomy (RC) who could not receive or declined cisplatin chemotherapy[^1]. Evaluated by blinded independent central review, patients receiving perioperative durvalumab with enfortumab vedotin-ejfv achieved statistically significant gains in both event-free survival (EFS) and overall survival (OS) compared with radical cystectomy alone[^1].

Median EFS was not reached in the combination arm (95% CI: 33.2 months to not reached) versus 12.1 months (95% CI: 7.6, 27.0) in the surgery-alone arm, yielding a hazard ratio of 0.51 (95% CI: 0.38, 0.67; two-sided p < 0.0001) — a 49% reduction in the risk of recurrence, progression, or death — while overall survival improved with a hazard ratio of 0.58 (95% CI: 0.40, 0.83; two-sided p = 0.0027)[^1]. For adults weighing at least 30 kg, recommended dosing comprises durvalumab 1,500 mg every three weeks paired with enfortumab vedotin-ejfv 1.25 mg/kg on Days 1 and 8 for three preoperative cycles, followed by single-agent durvalumab 1,500 mg every four weeks for up to nine postoperative cycles[^1].

## Project Orbis Seven-Agency Review Accelerates Perioperative PD-L1 and ADC Access

Conducted under Priority Review using the voluntary Assessment Aid within the FDA Oncology Center of Excellence's Project Orbis framework, the review coordinated concurrent regulatory evaluation across six international counterparts: Australia's Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA), Brazil's Health Regulatory Agency (ANVISA), Health Canada, Israel's Ministry of Health (IMoH), Singapore's Health Sciences Authority (HSA), and the United Kingdom's Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA)[^1].

Prescribing warnings highlight immune-mediated adverse reactions, infusion-related reactions, allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplant complications, and embryo-fetal toxicity, with common adverse reactions and laboratory abnormalities exceeding 20% incidence including elevated liver transaminases, decreased hemoglobin, rash, increased creatinine, pruritus, urinary tract infection, fatigue, and peripheral neuropathy[^1]. Pairing PD-L1 checkpoint blockade with an antibody-drug conjugate establishes a perioperative option for cisplatin-ineligible patients previously limited to cystectomy without neoadjuvant systemic therapy[^1].

## Global Model WHO Delegates Target 2027 Treaty Talks Over 16,000 Plastic Chemicals

While oncology regulators expanded targeted biologics, public health delegates in Geneva focused on upstream chemical exposure. As detailed in [WHO's November 13 departmental update](https://www.who.int/news/item/13-11-2026-put-health-in-the-treaty---youth-delegates-at-the-global-model-who-2026-adopt-declaration-on-plastics-and-health), the fourth Global Model WHO — organized with the World Federation of United Nations Associations from November 10 to 13 — convened delegates from all six WHO regions across six specialized committees, culminating in a declaration warning that more than 16,000 chemicals are used or present in plastics, at least one quarter of which are hazardous, with microplastic particles already detected in human blood, brain tissue, and amniotic fluid[^2].

Noting that the Intergovernmental Negotiating Committee Chair's August 2026 informal treaty text lacked a dedicated health article, the declaration directs 11 calls to action to the resumed global plastics treaty talks (INC-5.4) in March 2027 and the Eightieth World Health Assembly in May 2027[^2]. Key demands include legally binding health obligations across the plastic lifecycle, inclusion of health ministries on national negotiating teams, conflict-of-interest firewalls vis-à-vis petrochemical and plastics producers, measurable production cuts, phase-outs of hazardous chemicals, standardized human biomonitoring methods, occupational protections for waste pickers, and strictly time-limited medical-device exemptions prioritizing infants and pregnant patients[^2].
