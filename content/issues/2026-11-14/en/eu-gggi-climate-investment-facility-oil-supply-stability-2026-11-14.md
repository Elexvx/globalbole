On November 13 on the margins of COP31, the European Union and the Global Green Growth Institute (GGGI) [signed a €10 million contribution agreement establishing the Global Gateway Climate Investment Facility](https://international-partnerships.ec.europa.eu/news-and-events/news/eu-and-gggi-sign-eur10-million-contribution-agreement-mobilise-eur1-billion-climate-aligned-2026-11-13_en), a four-year programme designed to unlock at least €1 billion in climate-aligned capital across partner economies[^1]. On the same day in Brussels, the European Commission convened Member States, the International Energy Agency (IEA), the Energy Community, and NATO in the Oil Coordination Group, [confirming that crude oil and petroleum product supplies across the EU remain stable entering the winter heating season](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/news/oil-coordination-group-confirms-continued-supply-stability-across-eu-2026-11-13_en)[^2].

## Global Gateway Climate Investment Facility Targets €750 Million in Private Capital

According to the [Directorate-General for International Partnerships announcement](https://international-partnerships.ec.europa.eu/news-and-events/news/eu-and-gggi-sign-eur10-million-contribution-agreement-mobilise-eur1-billion-climate-aligned-2026-11-13_en), the €10 million technical assistance agreement was signed by Jan Dusík, Deputy Director-General for Climate Action at the European Commission, and Sang-Hyup Kim, Executive Director of GGGI[^1]. Running from 2026 through 2030 across at least 15 partner countries, the Facility aims to mobilize at least €1 billion in total climate-aligned investment, including €750 million from private-sector investors[^1].

European Commissioner for International Partnerships Jozef Síkela underscored that sustainable transition investments anchor economic growth under the EU's Global Gateway strategy, which has mobilized more than €300 billion in public and private investments across digital, energy, and transport corridors since 2021[^1]. GGGI Executive Director Sang-Hyup Kim noted that the institute has already helped governments mobilize over USD 18 billion in green finance worldwide and will leverage the EU partnership to convert national climate pledges into bankable pipelines[^1].

## Overcoming Project Preparation Bottlenecks and Advancing Paris Agreement Article 6 Readiness

Rather than funding standalone studies, the Facility addresses structural barriers that routinely stall climate capital in emerging markets: fragmented inter-ministerial planning, limited project preparation capacity, and hurdles in structuring blended finance[^1]. Technical teams will assist partner governments in designing investment-ready infrastructure aligned with national climate plans[^1].

The mandate places special emphasis on climate adaptation and resilience — areas chronically underserved by commercial finance — while building institutional readiness for partner countries to participate in international carbon credit markets under Article 6 of the Paris Agreement[^1].

## EU Oil Coordination Group Reviews Refining Dialogue and October Diesel Stock Releases

Complementing external green finance diplomacy, European energy regulators assessed near-term fuel security alongside industry representatives. As reported by the [Directorate-General for Energy on November 13](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/news/oil-coordination-group-confirms-continued-supply-stability-across-eu-2026-11-13_en), the Oil Coordination Group — bringing together Commission specialists, EU Member States, the IEA, the Energy Community, and NATO — concluded that EU oil supplies remain stable for the time being[^2].

Participants observed that while crude oil and refined product prices have moderated recently, they remain significantly above pre-conflict benchmarks amid tight global market balances requiring sustained vigilance[^2]. Delegates also mapped out next steps under the recently launched High-level Dialogue with the EU refining sector and reviewed EU Member States' substantial October contributions — particularly in diesel volumes — toward the March 2026 IEA collective action on emergency oil stock releases throughout the heating season[^2].
