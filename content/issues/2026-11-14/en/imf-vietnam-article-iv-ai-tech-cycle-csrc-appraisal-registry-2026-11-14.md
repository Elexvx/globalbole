On November 13, the International Monetary Fund (IMF) issued Press Release No. 26/361 following its November 3–13 [2026 Article IV consultation mission to Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/11/13/pr26361-vietnam-imf-staff-completes-2026-article-iv-mission), reporting that Vietnam's real GDP expanded 9.0% year-on-year in the first three quarters of 2026 on the strength of the global AI-driven technology cycle and robust investment[^1]. On the same day in Beijing, the China Securities Regulatory Commission (CSRC) published its [November 13 list of asset appraisal institutions cancelling securities service business filings](https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c100102/c7670715/content.shtml), updating market gatekeeper disclosures across five valuation firms[^2].

## AI Hardware Exports Lift Vietnam's 2026 Growth Forecast to 9.2% Amid Current Account Deficit

According to the [end-of-mission statement by IMF team leader Martin Sommer](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/11/13/pr26361-vietnam-imf-staff-completes-2026-article-iv-mission), Vietnam remains among the world's fastest-growing economies, with full-year real GDP projected to grow 9.2% in 2026 and 8.9% in 2027 — above estimated potential output[^1]. Headline inflation moderated to 5.1% in October, leaving the January–October average slightly above the government's 4.5% annual target, while elevated global oil and imported semiconductor prices pushed the current account into deficit even as strong foreign direct investment inflows and higher interest rates kept the exchange rate and foreign exchange reserves broadly stable[^1].

IMF staff cautioned that near-term risks remain tilted toward slower growth and persistent inflation, citing prolonged Middle East tensions, a potential market reassessment of global AI investment prospects, trade policy uncertainty, public investment outpacing domestic absorptive capacity, rapid credit expansion, and extreme weather shocks[^1].

## IMF Recommends 15% Credit Growth Anchor, Phased Energy Subsidy Exit, and Faster Basel III Transition

To safeguard macro-financial stability, the mission advised adopting a broadly neutral fiscal stance in 2027, pacing public investment toward high-return projects, phasing out broad energy price subsidies as global oil prices ease in favor of targeted transfers for vulnerable households, and tightening fiscal oversight over public-private partnerships and state-owned enterprises[^1]. Noting that rapid lending has strained bank funding and lifted interest rates — prompting liquidity injections by the State Bank of Vietnam (SBV) — IMF staff urged the central bank to hold year-end credit growth around 15%, reduce reliance on administrative credit ceilings, and permit greater exchange rate flexibility[^1].

On financial supervision, the statement called for accelerating banks' transition toward recently introduced Basel III capital and liquidity standards while avoiding regulatory forbearance, alongside structural reforms to build Vietnam's comprehensive multi-tier social security system by 2030 and upgrade national economic and financial statistics[^1].

## China's CSRC Announces Voluntary Deregistration of Five Securities Asset Appraisal Firms

In parallel with macroprudential calibration in Southeast Asia, Chinese securities regulators updated intermediary registry disclosures under the Measures for the Filing of Asset Appraisal Institutions Engaging in Securities Service Business (Cai Zi [2024] No. 172). As detailed in [CSRC's November 13 public filing notice](https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c100102/c7670715/content.shtml), five appraisal institutions completed voluntary cancellation of their securities service business registrations[^2].

The five deregistered entities are Beijing Zhongkehua Asset Appraisal Co., Ltd., Jiangsu Guoheng Zhongce Land and Real Estate Asset Appraisal Consulting Co., Ltd., Beijing Qianhai Real Estate and Land Asset Appraisal Co., Ltd., Sichuan Huakun Real Estate and Land Asset Appraisal Co., Ltd., and Beijing Colliers International Land and Real Estate Asset Appraisal Co., Ltd.[^2]. Routine publication of voluntary exits alongside active filings reinforces audit and valuation transparency for listed-company restructurings, real-estate appraisals, and capital market disclosures[^2].
