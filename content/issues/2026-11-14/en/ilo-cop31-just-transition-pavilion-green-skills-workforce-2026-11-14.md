On November 13 at COP31 in Antalya, Türkiye, the International Labour Organization (ILO) and the European Commission — partnering with the International Organisation of Employers (IOE), the International Trade Union Confederation (ITUC), and UNFCCC — [officially inaugurated the Just Transition Pavilion](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/just-transition-pavilion-opens-cop31-decent-work-and-social-justice-gain) to anchor decent work, green skills, and social protection inside global climate implementation[^1]. On the same day, UN Climate Change Executive Secretary Simon Stiell addressed the Seventh Biennial High-Level Ministerial Dialogue on Climate Finance, calling on governments to [deliver the $1.3 trillion Baku to Belém Roadmap in ways that free developing economies from compounding debt and disaster spirals](https://unfccc.int/news/un-climate-chief-at-cop31-today-climate-finance-must-help-countries-break-free)[^2].

## Tripartite Just Transition Pavilion Bridges Formal COP31 Tracks and National Workforce Strategies

According to [ILO's November 13 press release](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/just-transition-pavilion-opens-cop31-decent-work-and-social-justice-gain), the European Commission-funded Pavilion opened with the high-level session "From Climate Commitments to Decent Work: Advancing a Just Transition for All," bringing together ministers, employers' federations, trade unions, and civil society during the November 9–20 conference[^1]. Across the first week of negotiations, employment, skills development, social dialogue, and labour rights gained traction within the Just Transition Work Programme (JTWP), the Global Goal on Adaptation (GGA), and gender-and-climate workstreams[^1].

ILO Policy Hub Director Moustapha Gueye stressed that the central test now lies in translating negotiation text into national labour market policies covering green workforce readiness, occupational safety and health (OSH), youth employment, and sustainable enterprises[^1]. Alongside thematic panels, the Pavilion spotlights ILO technical assistance supporting national just transition strategies in Nigeria, Bangladesh, and host country Türkiye, which formally launched its National Just Transition Strategy ahead of COP31[^1].

## Simon Stiell Warns of Sovereign Debt-and-Disaster Loops Under the $1.3 Trillion Roadmap

Scaling green employment and adaptive social protection requires affordable capital across developing economies. In his [published remarks to the Seventh Biennial High-Level Ministerial Dialogue on Climate Finance](https://unfccc.int/news/un-climate-chief-at-cop31-today-climate-finance-must-help-countries-break-free) — the final session in the current cycle feeding into COP32 — Simon Stiell traced progress from Cancun's $100 billion pledge to the COP29 targets enshrined in the Baku to Belém Roadmap: $300 billion annually led by developed nations and $1.3 trillion per year from all sources for developing countries by 2035[^2].

Stiell warned, however, that headline volume targets alone fail if delivery terms deepen fiscal distress: recurring threats of floods, wildfires, storms, and droughts inflate sovereign borrowing spreads even before disasters strike, and once extreme weather hits, falling tax revenues and surging reconstruction bills drive public debt higher, leaving governments with fewer resources to invest in workforce and infrastructure resilience[^2].

## Four Delivery Pillars: Grant Concessionality, Simplified Access, and Domestic Development Banks

To replace that downward spiral with a virtuous cycle of stronger domestic institutions, lower capital costs, and low-emissions job growth, Stiell outlined four operational reforms: first, debt-responsive terms that pair private capital and guarantees with indispensable grant-based and highly concessional funding for Least Developed Countries (LDCs) and Small Island Developing States (SIDS); second, streamlined application rules across fragmented multilateral funding windows[^2].

Third, Stiell urged international funds to strengthen rather than bypass national treasuries, local governments, and domestic development banks; fourth, he called for deliberate sequencing where early grants build institutional pipelines and concessional risk-sharing instruments scale technology deployment[^2]. Together, the COP31 labour and finance discussions underscore that durable green job creation depends equally on tripartite skills governance at home and debt-sustainable climate capital abroad[^1][^2].
