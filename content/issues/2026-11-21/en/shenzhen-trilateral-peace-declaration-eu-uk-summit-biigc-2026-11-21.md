Between November 19 and November 20, 2026, high-level summitry in Shenzhen, Brussels, and Dublin produced three major diplomatic declarations addressing strategic stability, global energy supply, and European institutional cooperation. On November 19, the [Ministry of Justice of the People's Republic of China](https://www.moj.gov.cn/pub/sfbgw/gwxw/ttxw/202611/t20261119_542064.html) and [Chinese diplomatic missions](https://tr.china-embassy.gov.cn/zgyw/202611/t20261119_12063234.htm) published the full text of the *China-Russia-U.S. Heads of State Peace Declaration* following the first-ever trilateral summit chaired by President Xi Jinping with Russian President Vladimir Putin and U.S. President Donald Trump in Shenzhen. ## Shenzhen Trilateral Summit Produces Four-Point Peace Declaration on UN Reform, Refined Petroleum Supply, State Immunity, and Nuclear Risk Reduction

According to the [official summit readout published by the Chinese Embassy in Türkiye](https://tr.china-embassy.gov.cn/zgyw/202611/t20261119_12063234.htm), President Xi Jinping chaired the historic first trilateral meeting among the heads of state of China, Russia, and the United States in Shenzhen on November 19, 2026. During the talks, Xi outlined three guiding principles calling on major powers to act as guardians of world peace through dialogue and negotiation, drivers of global development by safeguarding stable industrial and supply chains, and upholders of the international order through genuine multilateralism. Presidents Putin and Trump concurred that Russia, the United States, and China bear special responsibilities as major powers to maintain strategic communication, prevent direct confrontation, and promote political settlements to regional crises[^2].

The resulting four-point [*China-Russia-U.S. Heads of State Peace Declaration* published by the Ministry of Justice of the PRC](https://www.moj.gov.cn/pub/sfbgw/gwxw/ttxw/202611/t20261119_542064.html) sets out concrete commitments across four pillars: first, as permanent members of the UN Security Council, the three nations pledge to uphold the UN-centered international system and advance necessary United Nations and Security Council reforms; second, to stabilize volatile global energy markets, the three countries plan to **increase the production and supply of refined petroleum products** and ensure secure, stable global energy flows; third, the declaration affirms that international judicial bodies must respect state consent and the immunity of state officials under international law, stating explicitly that the International Criminal Court (ICC) and similar bodies must not abuse jurisdiction; and fourth, the leaders reaffirm that **a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought**, initiating structured dialogue on nuclear risk reduction and non-proliferation[^1].

## Brussels Hosts Second EU-UK Summit as Dublin BIIGC Communiqué Advances Security Cooperation and Legacy Framework

In Brussels on November 20, the [European Council press release on the second EU–United Kingdom Summit](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/11/20/joint-statement-eu-united-kingdom-summit-20-november-2026/) confirmed that European Council President António Costa, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, and United Kingdom Prime Minister Andy Burnham met to review bilateral relations and adopted the *Joint Statement of the EU–United Kingdom Summit 2026*, reinforcing structured cooperation across foreign policy, security, and regulatory coordination[^3].

On the eve of the Brussels meeting, the [BIIGC Joint Communiqué issued in Dublin on November 19](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/biigc-joint-communique-november-2026) documented ministerial talks at Iveagh House co-chaired by Irish Minister for Foreign Affairs and Trade and Minister for Defence Helen McEntee TD, Irish Minister for Justice, Home Affairs and Migration Jim O'Callaghan TD, and UK Secretary of State for Northern Ireland Sir Chris Bryant MP (joined by Sarah Owen MP and Baroness Ruth Anderson). Ministers reviewed the operation of Good Friday Agreement institutions, legislative implementation of their Joint Framework on Northern Ireland legacy alongside the Omagh Bombing Inquiry, and cross-border counter-terrorism operations in County Monaghan and County Louth following New IRA attacks targeting Dunmurry and Lisburn police stations. The Conference also examined Independent Expert Fleur Ravensbergen's report on a formal scoping process for paramilitary transition to disbandment, marked the 25th anniversary of the Police Service of Northern Ireland (PSNI), and coordinated the UK–Ireland 2030 agenda and successor PEACE Programme ahead of the November 20 EU–UK Summit[^4].
