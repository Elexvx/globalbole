From November 19 to November 20, 2026, the International Labour Organization (ILO) issued three major updates addressing South-South labor cooperation, healthcare occupational safety, and legal protections against workplace violence. In Geneva on November 20, the [ILO and China's Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security (MOHRSS)](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/ilo-and-china-reaffirm-commitment-south-south-cooperation-decent-work-and) convened the 12th Joint Committee Meeting on ILO–China Cooperation to review five years of Asia-Pacific skills and social-protection initiatives and chart joint responses to artificial intelligence and the platform economy. The meeting followed November 19 dispatches marking [over a decade of ILO–WHO HealthWISE occupational safety deployment across Chinese hospitals](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/ilo-healthwise-tool-supports-decent-work-health-workers-china) and confirming that [the Netherlands ratified the Violence and Harassment Convention, 2019 (No. 190)](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/netherlands-ratifies-ilo-convention-violence-and-harassment-world-work)[^1][^2][^3].

## 12th ILO-China Joint Committee Aligns South-South Cooperation on AI and Platform Work with Decade-Long HealthWISE Hospital Rollout

According to the [ILO news release on the 12th Joint Committee Meeting in Geneva](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/ilo-and-china-reaffirm-commitment-south-south-cooperation-decent-work-and), delegations led by ILO Director-General Gilbert F. Houngbo and Chinese Vice Minister of Human Resources and Social Security Yan Qinghui assessed progress achieved since the previous Joint Committee session in 2021. Over the past five years, China-supported ILO technical cooperation has strengthened public employment services, digital and green vocational training institutions, and digitally enabled social-protection delivery across the Asia-Pacific region. Looking ahead, both sides agreed to expand South-South cooperation to help Global South economies harness artificial intelligence and the platform economy while mitigating worker risks, linking the ILO–China framework with the Global Development Initiative (GDI), the Belt and Road Initiative (BRI), and **China's 2027 presidency of the BRICS**[^1].

Within occupational safety and health (OSH), a separate [ILO report on the HealthWISE tool in China](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/ilo-healthwise-tool-supports-decent-work-health-workers-china) highlighted more than ten years of implementation since the joint ILO–WHO action manual was introduced in 2015. Across that decade, Chinese medical institutions conducted **12 HealthWISE training programmes** spanning Henan, Guangxi, Beijing, Guangdong, Hubei, and Xinjiang, reaching **over 1,200 healthcare professionals**. At the latest workshop held in Nanning, Guangxi from November 10 to 13, 2026—organized by Peking Union Medical College (PUMC) and hosted by Guangxi Medical University—practitioners including Attending Physician Si Huiliang of the First Affiliated Hospital of Guangxi Medical University reviewed workplace hazard controls alongside institutional rollouts at Nanning Fourth People's Hospital and the First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University[^2].

## Netherlands Becomes 58th Country Worldwide and 17th EU Member State to Ratify ILO Convention No. 190 on Workplace Violence

In European labor law enforcement, the [ILO announcement on the Netherlands' ratification of Convention No. 190](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/netherlands-ratifies-ilo-convention-violence-and-harassment-world-work) confirmed that the Dutch government formally deposited its instrument of ratification for the Violence and Harassment Convention, 2019 (No. 190), becoming the **58th ILO Member State worldwide** and the **17th European Union Member State** to ratify the treaty. As the first international labor standard affirming the right of everyone to a world of work free from violence and harassment—including gender-based violence—Convention No. 190 requires comprehensive national prevention frameworks covering employees, trainees, apprentices, volunteers, jobseekers, and third parties across formal and informal sectors[^3].

Underscoring the domestic evidence driving ratification, [Wilm Geurts, Director of International Affairs at the Dutch Ministry of Social Affairs and Employment](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/netherlands-ratifies-ilo-convention-violence-and-harassment-world-work), noted that **17 percent of employees in the Netherlands** experienced sexually transgressive behaviour at work in 2025. To turn the convention's obligations into workplace practice, the Dutch government is pairing its National Action Programme (2023–2026) with the *Sustainable Approach to Demanding Work* subsidy scheme—funding sectoral and company-level psychosocial risk reduction—and providing free e-learning modules to train managers in preventing undesirable workplace conduct[^3].
