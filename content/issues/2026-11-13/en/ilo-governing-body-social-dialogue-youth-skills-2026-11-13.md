Meeting in Geneva on November 12, the 358th Session of the International Labour Organization (ILO) Governing Body formally adopted its [decision on the follow-up plan of action for social dialogue and tripartism (GB.358/INS/4/1)](https://www.ilo.org/resource/record-decisions/gb/gb358/decision-concerning-follow-resolution-concerning-third-recurrent-discussion), directing the ILO Director-General to embed tripartite consultation priorities across upcoming programme and budget cycles[^1]. On the same day, the ILO reported outcomes from an [EU-supported international employment conference in Baghdad](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/international-conference-baghdad-highlights-how-decent-work-strengthens) uniting over 160 researchers from roughly 23 countries around youth school-to-work transitions[^2], while the Bank for International Settlements (BIS) published a [speech by Bank of France Governor Emmanuel Moulin](https://www.bis.org/speeches/20261112-competitiveness-and-resilience-financial-sector-oxymoron-or-complementarity) warning that sovereign bond yield pressures could tighten corporate and household credit unless fiscal deficits are brought below 5% of GDP[^3].

## ILO Governing Body Mandate Prioritizes Tripartism Through Budget Reprioritization

Recorded under Institutional Section item [GB.358/INS/4/1 paragraph 22](https://www.ilo.org/resource/record-decisions/gb/gb358/decision-concerning-follow-resolution-concerning-third-recurrent-discussion), the Governing Body instructed the Director-General to incorporate its guidance when executing the action plan giving effect to the conclusions of the third recurrent discussion on social dialogue and tripartism[^1].

Rather than mandating immediate assessed-contribution increases, the decision directs the ILO secretariat to draw on the action plan when formulating future programme and budget proposals while meeting financing requirements through internal reprioritization within existing budgets alongside renewed extrabudgetary resource mobilization[^1]. That budgetary architecture anchors collective bargaining, workplace consultation, and tripartite labour-market governance as operational priorities across member states navigating technological and industrial transitions[^1].

## Baghdad Conference Backed by EU BEIT Programme Confronts Three Youth Employment Bottlenecks

Illustrating tripartite coordination on the ground, the [ILO's November 12 Baghdad report](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/international-conference-baghdad-highlights-how-decent-work-strengthens) detailed conclusions from the two-day 12th Scientific and 4th International Conference at Bayt al-Hikma—held on November 10–11 in cooperation with the European Union, the ILO, and Iraq's Supreme Judicial Council[^2]. Supported by the EU-funded ILO programme 'Building Equitable and Inclusive Transformation: Towards Decent Work and Affordable Housing in Iraq (BEIT, 2024–2027)' and the Social Protection Programme for Iraq, the forum convened over 160 scholars from Iraq and roughly 22 other nations alongside the Ministry of Planning, the Iraqi Trade Union Council, and the Iraqi Industries Union[^2].

ILO Country Coordinator Ruby al-Salem identified three interconnected structural barriers impeding young people's transition from classrooms into productive careers: persistent mismatches between academic curricula and employer skill demands, insufficient formal job creation, and wide gaps in social security coverage and worker representation—compounded for young women by unpaid care duties, transport constraints, and workplace discrimination[^2]. Technical sessions paired EU and ILO specialists with national social partners to align vocational training directly with public employment programmes and contributory social protection[^2].

## BIS Publishes Bank of France Warning Linking Sovereign Fiscal Discipline to Real-Economy Credit

Complementing supply-side labour reforms, macroeconomic financing conditions remain decisive for private-sector job creation. In a [central banker's speech published by the BIS on November 12](https://www.bis.org/speeches/20261112-competitiveness-and-resilience-financial-sector-oxymoron-or-complementarity) (delivered at the ACPR conference in Paris), Bank of France Governor and ACPR Chairman Emmanuel Moulin observed that sovereign bond yields have climbed across advanced economies since summer amid Middle East inflation risks, shifting rate expectations, and heavy corporate bond issuance—pressures amplified in France by domestic budget uncertainty[^3].

While emphasizing that European banks and insurers have demonstrated strong capital and credit resilience since the 2022 rate cycle and 2023 banking turbulence, Moulin warned that sustained sovereign yield increases raise borrowing costs for both households and businesses, weighing directly on real-economy investment and employment growth[^3]. Adopting a national budget before year-end that brings France's government deficit below 5% of GDP is therefore essential, underscoring how durable employment expansion relies simultaneously on tripartite skill governance and credible fiscal anchors[^3].
