China’s Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, Ministry of Finance and National Development and Reform Commission have given approval in principle to comprehensive hydrogen application pilot plans for five city clusters. The notice, dated August 15 and published by MIIT on October 16, names Beijing–Tianjin–Hebei, the Greater Bay Area, Northeast China (including eastern Inner Mongolia)–Yangtze River Delta, Xinjiang–Chengdu–Chongqing Twin-City Economic Circle, and the Yellow River’s “Great Bend”–Central Plains. The pilots run for four years. The three departments will provide guidance and assess performance, with the results informing central-government financial rewards.[^1]

The newly published document approves specific cluster plans under the overarching pilot framework released in March. That earlier notice already set out selection procedures, application priorities and funding arrangements. The selection of the five clusters is therefore an implementation step, rather than the introduction of an entirely new framework in October. Nor does the four-year duration establish a common countdown from the publication date: the latest notice gives no precise start and end dates for individual clusters.[^1][^2]

## Beyond vehicles, into industrial hydrogen use

The overarching framework makes fuel-cell vehicles a common application across the clusters, alongside green ammonia and methanol, hydrogen substitution for chemical feedstocks, hydrogen metallurgy, hydrogen blending for combustion and other innovative uses. Transport priorities include medium- and heavy-duty vehicles, medium- and long-distance freight and cold-chain logistics. Industrial applications must connect hydrogen supply and technology choices with downstream demand. Green ammonia and methanol projects, for example, must establish dependable markets for their output and may not be used as a pretext for building coal-based ammonia or methanol projects. Hydrogen blending for combustion is conditional on safety and reliability.[^2]

The pilots consequently cannot be understood solely through vehicle or refuelling-station counts. The October notice calls for implementation of four sets of tasks: hydrogen technology and equipment development, industrial hydrogen projects, fuel-cell vehicle deployment and hydrogen refuelling stations. For local authorities, delivery therefore involves equipment and infrastructure as well as sustained industrial demand. These requirements do not establish that projects have already been completed or are generating commercial returns.[^1]

## A funding ceiling is not an allocation

The March framework provides for central-government performance-based financial rewards, capped at RMB 1.6 billion per cluster over the four-year pilot. Reward rates are linked to downstream product applications or hydrogen-use volumes in different settings. Funding is advanced and subsequently reconciled, with amounts calculated against performance. Selecting five clusters does not mean that RMB 8 billion has already been disbursed. The ceiling for a cluster is also not an entitlement for an individual city or company within it.[^2]

The latest notice further requires local authorities to deliver their own reward funding on time and transfer central rewards in accordance with the rules. Where progress falls short or local reward payments are delayed, the three departments may reduce or suspend central support. Funds must support comprehensive hydrogen applications; they cannot be diverted to balancing budgets, repaying government debt or settling overdue payments owed to businesses.[^1]

The framework also excludes duplicate applications for pilot rewards where the hydrogen application component of a project has already received support through ultra-long special treasury bonds, central-budget investment, local-government special-purpose bonds or other such fiscal funding. Existing public support for the same application component therefore needs to be checked, rather than simply adding programmes together when estimating subsidies available to a business.[^2]

## Price targets have a date and a geographic scope

The framework targets an average end-use hydrogen price below RMB 25 per kilogram in the clusters by 2030, with an ambition to reach around RMB 15 in some areas with favourable conditions. It also seeks to double the national fuel-cell vehicle fleet from its 2025 level, aiming for 100,000 vehicles. These are policy objectives, not current market prices or achieved fleet figures. In particular, the RMB 15 ambition applies only to certain advantaged areas, not uniformly across China.[^2]

Several cluster names encompass cross-regional groupings. The latest notice accordingly stresses distinctive local strengths and coordination, while warning against local protectionism, low-level duplication and attempts to build a complete, self-contained system everywhere. Participating cities must also assign responsibility for safety management, produce annual self-assessment reports and evaluate economic, social and environmental benefits.[^1]

The next test is how local authorities turn approved plans into specific projects, stable supply-and-demand relationships and verifiable application outcomes. The assessment and funding mechanisms indicate an intention to use real applications to drive down costs. The notice alone, however, cannot establish which technology will prevail, which companies will benefit or how much each cluster will ultimately receive. Those judgments will require local implementation information and subsequent performance results.
