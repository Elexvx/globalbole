## Wind and Solar Acceleration Reshapes China's October Power Mix as Natural Gas Rebounds

According to the [NBS October energy production bulletin](https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202611/t20261116_1965837.html), total electricity generation by industrial enterprises above designated size stood at 794.0 billion kilowatt-hours in October, down 2.1% year-on-year at a daily average of 25.61 billion kilowatt-hours, while cumulative January-October output rose 1.8% to 8,290.4 billion kilowatt-hours[^1]. Clean power sources accelerated sharply: October wind generation jumped 33.7% year-on-year—speeding up by 23.1 percentage points from September—and solar generation expanded 31.0%, up 11.5 percentage points from the prior month, complemented by 17.5% nuclear growth as thermal and hydropower fell 7.3% and 18.9%, respectively[^1].

Upstream fuel extraction maintained steady pre-winter supply buffers. Raw coal production totaled 380 million tonnes in October, down 5.8% year-on-year as the pace of contraction narrowed by 1.0 percentage point from September, bringing ten-month coal output to 3.83 billion tonnes[^1]. Crude oil output increased 2.3% year-on-year to 18.45 million tonnes while refinery throughput fell 8.1% to 58.30 million tonnes, and natural gas output reached 22.2 billion cubic meters—rising 0.8% year-on-year to reverse September's 1.0% decline[^1].

## Federal Reserve Examines Advanced Computing Debt and $3 Trillion in Off-Balance-Sheet Power and Lease Obligations

While grid operators expand clean generation, financial supervisors are scrutinizing how data center power demand is financed. Speaking at the Seventh Annual ACM International Conference on Artificial Intelligence in Finance in Milan, Governor Barr summarized findings from the Federal Reserve's November Financial Stability Report in his [published remarks on AI capital structures](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/barr20261116a.htm), noting that on-balance-sheet debt across the seven largest AI firms is projected to surpass $800 billion before year-end 2026 as hyperscaler capital outlays approach the trajectory of the post-1995 telecom broadband boom[^2].

Beyond balance-sheet borrowing, the report estimates that maximum nominal off-balance-sheet obligations—encompassing long-term energy supply contracts, credit support for public utility grid investments, multi-year data center and compute leases, and hardware residual value guarantees—now exceed $3 trillion[^2]. Hyperscalers and major graphics processing unit (GPU) manufacturers have also deployed more than $100 billion in cumulative equity investments into large language model labs and cloud start-ups that use the capital to rent or buy compute from those same suppliers, alongside collateral resale guarantees backing asset-based financings for lower-credit developers[^2].

## Stranded Substation Risk, Rapid Chip Depreciation, and Minute-Scale Autonomous Deposit Runs

Comparing the AI buildout with 1870s railroads, mid-twentieth-century highways, and 1990s fiber-optic networks, Barr observed in his [Federal Reserve address](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/barr20261116a.htm) that specialized AI chips represent the bulk of data center capital costs and face rapid obsolescence risk even though lease rates on older chip vintages have held up thus far[^2]. Turning to physical power assets—including substations, high-voltage lines, and grid interconnections—he cautioned that if regional AI compute demand falls short of expectations, excess electrical capacity in certain locations could take years to find alternative users, echoing unlit dark fiber after the late-1990s telecom bust[^2].

Barr emphasized that opaque off-balance-sheet utility and lease commitments, combined with compressed equity risk premiums and elevated hedge-fund leverage via prime brokerages, could amplify fire sales if investor return timelines are repriced[^2]. Extending his warning to banking operations, he noted that major banks have already detected autonomous AI agents attempting unverified actions in retail accounts and cautioned that yield-seeking AI agents could execute deposit transfers simultaneously, triggering liquidity runs within minutes that outpace traditional supervisory intervention[^2].
