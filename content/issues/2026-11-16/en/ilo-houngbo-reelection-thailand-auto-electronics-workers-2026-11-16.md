On November 16, the Governing Body of the International Labour Organization (ILO) met at its 358th Session in Geneva and [re-elected Gilbert F. Houngbo of Togo as ILO Director-General](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/gilbert-f-houngbo-re-elected-director-general-international-labour) for a second five-year mandate running from October 1, 2027 to September 30, 2032, placing artificial intelligence's workplace impact and international labour standard supervision at the center of his agenda[^1]. In parallel across Southeast Asia's manufacturing belt, the ILO reported from Chachoengsao that it has [expanded worker-centered human rights due diligence training in Thailand's automotive and electronics sectors](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/ilo-strengthens-workers%E2%80%99-role-human-rights-due-diligence-thailand%E2%80%99s-auto), strengthening shop-floor risk monitoring across supply chains employing approximately 1.45 million workers[^2].

## Secret Ballot Delivers 51 Votes in Favor as Houngbo Prioritizes AI Governance and Tripartite Dialogue

According to the [ILO's official Geneva election release](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/gilbert-f-houngbo-re-elected-director-general-international-labour), Houngbo secured 51 votes in favor with five abstentions among the Governing Body's 56 titular voting members—comprising 28 government, 14 employer, and 14 worker representatives—following the pre-vote withdrawal of fellow candidates Yolanda Diaz Perez of Spain and Javier Gonzalez-Olaechea Franco of Peru after public dialogues in September and private hearings on November 9[^1]. Leading the UN's oldest specialized agency of 187 Member States since October 2022, Houngbo serves as the ILO's 11th Director-General and the first African leader to hold the post[^1].

Having established the Global Coalition for Social Justice and expanded decent-work initiatives in the digital economy during his first term, Houngbo pledged in his second mandate to reinforce standard-setting and supervisory machinery, deepen field operations, safeguard long-term financial sustainability, and address artificial intelligence's structural impact on employment[^1]. Speaking after the vote, he emphasized that tripartism remains the organization's vital strength in a fractured global environment, calling on governments, employers, and workers to build pragmatic compromises in pursuit of social justice[^1].

## Thai Automotive and Electronics Unions Advance Bipartite Shop-Floor Due Diligence

Translating international labour standards into industrial supply chains, the ILO convened an expanded two-day Human Rights Due Diligence (HRDD) training on November 14-15 in Chachoengsao in partnership with the Confederation of Thai Electrical Appliances, Electronic, Automobile and Metalworkers (TEAM). As detailed in the [ILO Thailand programme dispatch](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/ilo-strengthens-workers%E2%80%99-role-human-rights-due-diligence-thailand%E2%80%99s-auto), the initiative operates under the Advancing Responsible Supply Chains and Decent Work in Asia (RSC) project backed by Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI), building upon an initial June 2026 rollout that engaged over 50 enterprises[^2].

More than 40 trade unionists and worker representatives from automotive, auto-parts, and electronics factories across Thailand's Eastern Economic Corridor (EEC) and neighboring manufacturing provinces trained on identifying shop-floor labour risks overlooked by top-down audits, co-designing preventive and corrective steps, and ensuring reprisal-free grievance channels[^2]. Sessions specifically examined differentiated vulnerabilities affecting migrant, contract, and women workers, framing HRDD as a continuous bipartite dialogue rather than a one-time management compliance checklist[^2].

## Embedding Remedy Access Across a 1.45-Million-Worker Transition to Electric Vehicle Manufacturing

TEAM President Laray Youpensuk observed in the [ILO field report](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/ilo-strengthens-workers%E2%80%99-role-human-rights-due-diligence-thailand%E2%80%99s-auto) that pairing employers' strategic authority with workers' direct knowledge of production-line realities strengthens both workplace rights and factory productivity[^2]. ILO Project Technical Officer Mattie Milliken noted that under the ILO Tripartite Declaration of Principles concerning Multinational Enterprises and Social Policy (MNE Declaration), credible corporate due diligence requires consulting workers' organizations from the earliest stages of risk prioritization rather than merely notifying staff after decisions are finalized[^2].

Thailand's automotive and electronics industries employ roughly 1.45 million workers and anchor dense domestic and multinational supplier networks now pivoting toward electric and zero-emission vehicles[^2]. Building on the November workshop, the ILO's RSC programme will collaborate with union representatives through March 2027 to develop practical enterprise-level tools that expand workplace access to remedy, collective bargaining, and early dispute resolution as global supply chain due diligence mandates tighten[^2].
