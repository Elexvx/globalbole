## Around 30% of jobs in Hampshire and the Solent face AI task changes, led by 55,000 high-exposure workers

The University of Southampton published two companion reports on November 5, 2026, at the Business South Annual Conference: *How might artificial intelligence affect the labour market in Hampshire and the Solent* and *Fifty ways Hampshire and the Solent are shaping Britain’s AI revolution*. The labour study estimates that roughly **3 in 10 jobs (about 30%)** across Hampshire and the Solent are in occupations poised to change because of AI—slightly above the England average—while around **55,000 workers** (roughly **5%** of the regional workforce) hold roles where AI could take over a large share of daily tasks. Those high-exposure workers are **disproportionately female, part-time, and in the middle of their careers**. Finance, professional services, information and communications technology, and public administration face the largest shifts, whereas construction, hospitality, and care roles built on physical presence are least directly exposed.[^1]

At the same time, the reports document rapid regional AI adoption and enterprise formation: the share of local businesses reporting AI use doubled over three years, and the region now hosts more than **200** AI companies, including **57 new AI firms created in 2025 alone**. The University of Southampton leads the **£31 million** Responsible AI UK consortium, while local public-sector deployments include an AI virtual assistant at Hampshire Police and AI-assisted breast screening imaging at University Hospital Southampton NHS Foundation Trust. To prevent uneven displacement, the authors recommend expanded AI skills training, a public-sector exemplar programme, and a **transition guarantee** to retrain and redeploy at-risk staff.[^1]

## Americans ask 50 million daily health questions on Copilot and Bing, but healthcare deserts and broadband gaps persist

On the same day (November 5, 2026), the NAACP and Sanofi unveiled the third installment of their *ACE Your Health* StoryMap series—titled *AI Fluency is a Challenge on Both Sides of the Exam Room*—at the AfroTech Conference 2026 in Houston. Drawing on national datasets and responses from nearly **23,000 community members** across **47 states, the District of Columbia, and Puerto Rico**, the report notes that Americans ask Microsoft Copilot and Bing approximately **50 million** health-related questions per day and that **81% of physicians** already use AI in their work. Among respondents using AI for health information, **19%** reported doing so because they could not afford to see a healthcare provider, and Black adults were significantly more likely than White adults to turn to AI for mental health advice.[^2]

However, structural access and trust barriers limit equitable clinical benefits. The NAACP report finds that **1 in 3 Americans** lives in a healthcare desert—including all **154,000** Black residents of the Mississippi Delta, who live more than **25 miles** from a four- or five-star hospital—while roughly **3.8 million** Black Americans live in households without a broadband internet subscription. Nearly **two-thirds** of Black adults view racial bias as a major problem in healthcare, and **one-third** expect AI to increase bias in clinical settings. Both November 5 studies require careful methodological framing: the University of Southampton’s 55,000 figure measures **occupational task exposure** rather than projected job losses, and the NAACP report synthesizes community survey data with external secondary benchmarks under a partnership co-sponsored by Sanofi.[^1][^2]
