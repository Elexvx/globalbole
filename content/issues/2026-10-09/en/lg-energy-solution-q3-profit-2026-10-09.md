## Profit needs a cash-flow cross-check

A profitable quarter and a business that funds itself are different things. The SEC’s guide to financial statements explains the split. An income statement records earnings over a period. A cash-flow statement tracks money received and money paid. Depreciation reduces accounting income without being a cash payment in the current period. Changes in operating assets and liabilities also help explain the gap. Buying machinery appears in investing cash flow. Borrowing, and repaying loans, belongs with financing activities.[^3]

For a battery producer, the questions change with that split. Does operating cash cover investment in equipment? Are inventories or receivables absorbing cash? Is cash rising because of the business, or because of new financing? These are checks to apply to the fuller accounts. They are not findings about this quarter. Subtracting a single support item cannot establish that the rest of earnings are recurring. That conclusion requires the other adjustments to be identified, and their accounting treatment to be clear. The notes on accounting policies are essential to a meaningful comparison.[^3]

## Factory capacity is not delivered output

The IEA’s 2026 outlook puts global lithium-ion battery nameplate manufacturing capacity above 4 TWh at the end of 2025. Separately, it estimates EV battery deployment at 1.2 TWh during 2025, representing more than 70% of total battery deployment. The figures describe different things. One is the potential annual production rate of factories at year-end. The other is batteries actually deployed, in one end-use, during the year. Dividing one by the other would not produce a valid industry utilisation rate.[^4]

The same split applies when an expansion is announced. A new plant adds productive potential. Delivered, saleable batteries are what link the plant to customers. The IEA identifies manufacturing experience, specialised workers and access to equipment suppliers as important parts of a successful production ramp-up. Its broader assessment is that domestic cell factories also need reliable component supply chains. A factory’s location alone does not establish that its inputs are produced locally, or that its production economics are competitive.[^4]

## Chemistry and customer mix change the business

Battery manufacturing is not a single interchangeable activity. The IEA describes three main cell formats: cylindrical, pouch and prismatic. Pouch cells allow flexible dimensions and high energy density. Prismatic cells provide a rigid structure that can be packed efficiently. Chemistry, pack design and established customer relationships all influence which format is used. A change in market focus has to be read together with the product and the manufacturing choices behind it.[^4]

The report also places lithium iron phosphate, or LFP, above 55% of global EV battery deployment in 2025. That industry-wide mix does not describe any individual manufacturer’s sales. A general increase in battery demand is still a weak basis for predicting a supplier’s results. The demand has to match that supplier’s technology, its customers and its production footprint. The IEA’s assessment stresses that diversifying cell production, without developing the materials industry around it, leaves supply-chain dependencies in place.[^4]

## Storage requires both power and duration

For stationary batteries, the size of an order needs more than one unit. EIA distinguishes power capacity, measured in megawatts, from energy capacity, measured in megawatt-hours. Their ratio gives the duration at rated output. Its explainer distinguishes short bursts used to support grid stability from longer discharge cycles that move electricity from one time of day to another.[^5]

Two storage projects with the same power rating can require different amounts of stored energy. The service the battery is meant to provide helps determine the requirement. A megawatt headline does not establish it. EIA describes daily-cycling systems that store midday solar production for evening demand, whereas some grid services need responses lasting only seconds or minutes. These are technical distinctions. They are not a forecast of any supplier’s orders or margins. Storage demand and a manufacturer’s profitability still have to be assessed separately.[^5]

## The fuller picture requires several reconciliations

A useful reading of the next accounts connects three layers: reported earnings, the cash the business generates, and the operation that supplies the batteries. The financial statements can show how earnings translate into cash and how investment is funded. Operating disclosures can show which products and which customers account for the activity. Industry statistics provide context. They cannot fill gaps in a company’s own disclosure.[^3][^4]

Evidence of a durable improvement would be stronger if those layers moved together: better operating performance accompanied by cash generation, productive assets serving actual demand, and clear explanations of unusual items. A large earnings number alone cannot answer all three questions. A manufacturing challenge described for the sector as a whole does not prove that a particular company’s quarter has deteriorated. The task is to connect evidence that can be compared, without treating preliminary figures, technical capacity and cash actually received as interchangeable measures.[^3][^4]
