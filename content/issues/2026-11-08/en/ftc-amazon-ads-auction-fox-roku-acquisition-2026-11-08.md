## Unsealed Amazon Ads complaint details hidden second-price auction surcharges and $40 million revenue-gap emails

The U.S. Federal Trade Commission (FTC) and the attorneys general of **22 states**—including California, New York, Washington, Florida and Illinois—filed a newly unredacted complaint in the U.S. District Court for the Western District of Washington detailing allegations that Amazon secretly manipulated its online search advertising auctions with hidden surcharges to hit internal financial targets.[^1] Separately, Fox Corporation filed Form 8-K (Exhibit 99.1) with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) announcing that it completed its acquisition of connected-TV platform Roku, Inc. (Nasdaq: ROKU) on November 6, 2026, with Roku shares ceasing independent Nasdaq trading upon closing.[^2]

While the FTC and state partners originally filed suit in August 2026, the newly unsealed complaint quantifies the price increases imposed on advertisers through algorithmic reserve mechanisms.[^1] According to the unredacted allegations, Amazon represented its search ad marketplace as a standard second-price auction while covertly adding hidden surcharges that raised ad prices by up to **50%** above auction results on ordinary days and by up to **250%** during peak shopping events such as Prime Day and Black Friday.[^1] During the 2021 holiday season, these surcharges allegedly raised advertisers’ average cost-per-click (CPC) by **20%**—with some advertisers experiencing increases above **200%**—while during the 2022 holiday shopping season Amazon secretly raised surcharges to generate **$87 million** in additional global revenue; on Prime Day 2023, one major food and beverage advertiser faced price increases exceeding **100%** that internal Amazon assessments acknowledged were “destroying campaign performance.”[^1]

## Fox closes Roku acquisition across 100 million households with $400 million synergy target and integration risks

The unsealed filing further alleges that in 2023 Amazon executives used hidden auction surcharges to close corporate financial gaps, citing an internal email in which the head of Amazon Ads asked the senior vice president of ad products and technology to use reserve mechanisms to find at least **$40 million** (`$40MM`) to close a gap to plan.[^1] When advertisers asked account managers whether the second-price auction process had changed by design or mistake, Amazon staff allegedly gave false assurances that there was no bug or change in auction style and blamed higher CPCs solely on a more crowded marketplace.[^1] These figures represent plaintiffs’ allegations in the unredacted federal complaint, which remains in active litigation without a final judicial ruling.[^1]

In the connected-TV distribution market, Fox Corporation’s Form 8-K filing confirms that Roku stockholders received **$95.30** in cash and **0.9799** shares of Fox Class A common stock for each Roku Class A and Class B share, leaving existing Fox stockholders with approximately **74%** and former Roku stockholders with approximately **26%** of the combined company.[^2] Roku founder Anthony Wood joined the Fox board and became Co-Chair of the newly formed FOX Digital Platforms division—alongside Co-Chair and CEO Paul Cheesbrough—encompassing Roku’s ecosystem of more than **100 million** households worldwide, Tubi and FOX One.[^2] Management projects approximately **$400 million** in run-rate cost synergies within two years of closing and free-cash-flow-per-share accretion by the second full year, though the filing’s forward-looking disclosures enumerate 18 operational, debt-deleveraging and partner-retention risks that remain subject to post-merger execution.[^2]
