## Currency translation and asset-price valuation mechanics behind the monthly reserve change

China’s State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and the People’s Bank of China (PBOC) released official reserve asset statistics on November 7, 2026, showing that China’s foreign exchange reserves stood at **$3,405.2 billion** (`34,052` hundred million USD) at the end of October 2026, up by **$4.9 billion** or **0.14%** from the end of September and remaining above the $3.4 trillion mark for a seventh consecutive month.[^1][^2] Concurrent official reserve statistics showed that China’s official gold reserves reached 78.30 million fine troy ounces (approximately 2,435.4 metric tons) at the end of October, an increase of **830,000 ounces** (roughly 25 to 26 tons) from September’s 77.47 million ounces, marking the 24th consecutive month of central-bank gold additions.[^2][^3]

Addressing the modest October increase in headline foreign exchange reserves, SAFE officials stated in the release that global macroeconomic data and shifting monetary policy expectations across major economies drove the US dollar index higher in October 2026 while global financial asset prices were mixed, with the combined effect of currency translation and asset-price changes lifting the headline reserve total.[^1][^3] On the current-account side, high-tech manufacturing exports continued to underpin external balance resilience, with China’s integrated circuit exports rising **99.1%** year-on-year during the first three quarters of 2026 amid sustained global capital expenditure on artificial intelligence infrastructure.[^2]

## Two full years of official gold accumulation and reserve-portfolio diversification

Interpreting monthly foreign exchange reserve movements requires separating dollar-denominated book valuation shifts from actual cross-border capital flows or central-bank foreign exchange intervention. Because China’s reserve portfolio holds sovereign debt and liquid financial assets denominated in US dollars, euros, yen and pounds sterling but reports the aggregate value in US dollars, a stronger US dollar index mechanically reduces the dollar value of non-dollar holdings. Recording a $4.9 billion net gain despite dollar appreciation indicates that coupon income and price gains across portions of the global fixed-income and financial asset portfolio more than offset negative currency-translation effects; the 0.14% monthly change therefore reflects mark-to-market portfolio revaluation rather than a direct measure of net foreign capital inflows.[^1][^2]

Unlike the mark-to-market valuation swings in foreign exchange reserves, changes in official gold reserves track physical tonnage additions. The 830,000-ounce purchase in October 2026 completed a full two-year (24-month) streak of uninterrupted monthly additions totaling roughly **5.50 million ounces** (about 171 metric tons).[^2] Analysts cited by People’s Daily and commentary from the World Gold Council note that amid elevated geopolitical uncertainty, expanding sovereign debt burdens and volatile bullion prices, steady gold accumulation helps diversify official reserve assets away from single-currency sovereign credit exposure and strengthens portfolio resilience without counterparty risk; however, the PBOC discloses only month-end ounce totals without reporting execution prices or target ceilings, meaning the streak reflects structural reserve management rather than a short-term price signal.[^2]
