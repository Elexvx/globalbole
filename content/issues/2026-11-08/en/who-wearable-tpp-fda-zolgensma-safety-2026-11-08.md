## WHO draft Target Product Profile for wrist-worn wearables: bridging consumer sensors and national health surveillance

The World Health Organization (WHO) launched a public consultation on November 8, 2026—running through **December 6, 2026** at 12:00 UTC—on a draft Target Product Profile (TPP) for wrist-worn wearable devices intended to measure physical activity and sedentary behavior within national and subnational population health monitoring systems.[^1] Earlier the same week, on November 6, 2026, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) issued a biologics safety communication announcing an investigation into postmarketing reports of malignancies in spinal muscular atrophy (SMA) patients treated with the adeno-associated virus (AAV) vector gene therapy Zolgensma, including confirmed cases of product-related insertional oncogenesis.[^2]

In its November 8 consultation notice, WHO noted that while physical inactivity and sedentary behavior are major risk factors for noncommunicable diseases and poor mental health, objective wearable-based monitoring remains sparse in routine public health surveillance, particularly across low- and middle-income countries (LMICs).[^1] The draft TPP sets out both minimum and enhanced specifications for fit-for-purpose wrist-worn sensors alongside broader operational requirements for open data access, cross-country comparability and sustainable deployment in resource-constrained settings.[^1] The document is currently an open consultation draft rather than a binding procurement list or regulatory approval standard, with stakeholder feedback used to inform final consensus revisions after December 6.[^1]

## FDA investigation into Zolgensma insertional oncogenesis: molecular evidence of AAV integration at the GLI1 locus

In advanced biologics oversight, the FDA’s safety notice marks a transition from theoretical vector-integration warnings to molecularly confirmed clinical adverse events.[^2] FDA reported receiving postmarketing adverse event reports of tumors—spanning the central and peripheral nervous systems, spinal/paraspinal regions and kidney—in pediatric SMA patients treated with Zolgensma (onasemnogene abeparvovec, approved May 24, 2019 for intravenous infusion in children under 2 years of age).[^2] Crucially, **three cases** involved tumors associated with the **GLI1** (Glioma-Associated Oncogene Homolog 1) oncogene; in **two of those cases**, molecular analysis of tumor tissue confirmed that Zolgensma AAV vector DNA integrated into the host genome with the construct’s promoter region inserted upstream of or within the GLI1 oncogene, while the mechanism in the third case remains under investigation.[^2]

Citing two additional 2026 *New England Journal of Medicine* reports of AAV integration-driven tumors in other programs—a GLI1 integration event in the FBX-101 program (published October 15, 2026) and a PLAG1 integration event in the RGX-111 program (published June 4, 2026)—the FDA determined that the neoplasia risk applies to both onasemnogene abeparvovec products: Zolgensma and the intrathecally administered Itvisma (approved November 24, 2025 for SMA patients aged 2 years and older, which has no reported neoplasia cases to date) because both share the same vector and construct.[^2] The FDA emphasized that the overall benefits of Zolgensma and Itvisma continue to outweigh their risks for approved indications and has not suspended clinical use, but is evaluating prescribing-label updates and long-term follow-up requirements.[^2]
