## Puerto Rico trunk lines operating at up to 70% derating: the 400-mile right-of-way clearing mandate through February 2027

The U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) issued two renewed Federal Power Act Section 202(c) emergency orders—including DOE Order No. 202-25-2F—taking effect on **November 8, 2026** through **February 5, 2027**, authorizing the Puerto Rico Electric Power Authority (PREPA) to dispatch critical generation units and mandating accelerated transmission right-of-way vegetation clearing for the island’s **3.2 million** residents.[^1][^2] Separately, on November 6, 2026, the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) Department of Nuclear Energy published findings from the **15th anniversary** technical meeting of the International Network of Laboratories for Nuclear Waste Characterization (LABONET), bringing together **74 specialists** from **42 countries** to evaluate measurement validation and inventory methods for legacy and decommissioning waste.[^3]

According to the text of DOE Order No. `202-25-2F`, overgrown vegetation along transmission corridors has forced portions of Puerto Rico’s trunk grid to operate with **up to 70% derating** to prevent heated lines from sagging into trees and triggering faults or wildfires.[^2] Engineering estimates cited in the order indicate that full vegetation clearing across the **115 kV** and **230 kV** transmission rights-of-way could reduce adverse outage events by **over 80%**.[^2] The order directs PREPA and its agents to clear at least an additional **400 miles** of designated lines by February 5, 2027 and submit biweekly Wednesday compliance reports using the July 8, 2025 DOE reporting templates; however, the order notes that prior vegetation-management delays have already postponed necessary repairs to degraded transmission and distribution hardware needed to stabilize voltage and frequency regulation.[^2]

## Fifteen years of IAEA LABONET: scaling factors, non-destructive assay and disposal acceptance limits for legacy nuclear waste

In the nuclear back-end sector, the IAEA report details outcomes from the Technical Meeting on Advanced Techniques for Radioactive Waste Characterization held in Szeged, Hungary (hosted by ELI-ALPS and ISOTOPTECH, alongside a site visit to Hungary’s Bátaapáti National Radioactive Waste Repository), focusing on how physical, chemical and radiological measurements determine whether waste packages meet repository acceptance limits.[^3] For older legacy waste with incomplete historical records or uneven radionuclide distribution, specialists examined gamma scanning, 3D tomography, X-ray diffraction for crystalline phases and laser-based imaging to identify internal porosity and structural defects inside sealed waste drums.[^3]

To quantify radionuclides that are difficult to measure directly from outside a container, participants evaluated the use of “scaling factors”—established ratios between easily measured gamma emitters and hard-to-measure isotopes—alongside operational case studies from Ukraine’s Rivne Nuclear Power Plant, Lithuania’s Maisiagala historical storage retrieval, and digital inventory systems in Canada and Brazil.[^3] LABONET Chairman Leonardus van Velzen emphasized that scaling factors require representative sampling and periodic recalibration whenever reactor operating conditions or waste streams shift, ahead of the IAEA’s International Conference on Advancement in Radioactive Waste Management scheduled for **October 11–15, 2027** in Vienna.[^3]
