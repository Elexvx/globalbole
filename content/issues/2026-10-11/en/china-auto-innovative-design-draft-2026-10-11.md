China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, with the public security and ecology ministries and the market regulator, published a draft notice on October 10 covering vehicle-design innovation and development testing. Comments are open from October 10 to November 10. The requirements have not been finalised. Any binding obligation will depend on the text that is eventually published.[^1]

## From demonstrating features to establishing safe use

From January 1, 2027, newly submitted products involving innovative designs would have to include additional technical parameters.[^2]

If that approach is adopted, a feature would not be signed off on the demonstration alone. The filing would also have to set out the conditions it runs under, how it was validated, and what it does when it fails. A video can show normal operation. A malfunction, an operating error or an emergency is a different test.

For the teams building the cars, the check is more useful while the design is still open than as paperwork once a feature is fixed. Catch a design that could affect emergency operation early, and the structure, the interfaces and the user instructions can still be brought into line. This is a reading of how the proposed approval would work. It is not a claim that every manufacturer already follows the same process.

## Different functions require separate assessments

The proposals would prohibit fully concealed handles, require physical emergency controls, and disable zero-gravity, rotating and bed-mode seats while driving.[^3]

Folding and flexible displays would also be prohibited.[^4]

The hardware, the way a function is used, and the state of the vehicle are not one subject. Fold them into a single verdict and buyers can be misled, while the engineering risk that needs the work disappears from view.

At the driving controls the plainer test is this: can a critical action still be found and carried out when the driver is stressed, the light changes, or the usual control is gone? A function that changes seating posture is one case at rest, parked, and another on the road. Safety and convenience can be weighed that way. A count of features, or of how novel they look, cannot.

## Testing also puts development schedules under scrutiny

The proposed minimums are 30,000-kilometre reliability trials and one-year validation of environmental adaptability.[^5]

Kilometres and calendar time can be audited. They do not say which conditions were covered, how a failure was recorded, or whether a later change was tested again. Reaching the end of the distance does not mean every risk has gone. Failing a test does not mean the design can never work. What counts is whether a problem can be found, explained and corrected.

A manufacturer setting a launch against that standard has to put the date next to the evidence it will actually have. More testing could move the launch, and it could also cut later rework. What it costs and what it saves depend on the product and on implementation. The draft cannot establish any particular manufacturer's future profit.

## The final text and implementation still matter

The direction is visible. The obligation is not. How comments are taken in, whether clauses change, and which products are covered will depend on later official texts. An owner should not read a consultation as a requirement to replace a component already on the vehicle.

A new feature's convenience still has to be matched by safety that can be shown. Clearer operating limits, tests that uncover real risks, a product that is easier to operate when something fails: those are the results that would count. Counting additional application fields would not.
