An October 10 update put urban employment additions for January–September at 10.52 million, 87.7% of the annual target.[^1]

Officials are not stopping at the running total. They are also asking where the jobs come from, how services reach workers, and whether skills match what employers want. The State Council's employment-first plan for the 15th Five-Year Plan period, issued on June 11, already puts service-sector hiring, employment services and vocational training in one framework.[^4]

## From aggregate data to employment experiences

Surveyed urban unemployment averaged 5.2% over January–August 2026.[^2]

A figure for the year does not describe the labour market, and neither describes whether the jobs are any good. Age, industry and city change the picture again. The national average does not carry those breakdowns.

## Service-sector expansion already has specific industrial directions

The ministry will work with other departments on a document promoting employment through service-sector development.[^3]

The June plan from the State Council is specific about the sectors. It names domestic services, eldercare and childcare for expansion, and it wants richer cultural, tourism and sports offerings, local restaurant brands, industry clusters, and more employment in accommodation and catering. Business services on the same list run through scientific and technical services, modern logistics, software and information services, and supply-chain finance, with the stress on specialisation and on drawing in skilled workers.[^4]

Those fields do not hire in the same way. Household care turns on trust, skills and continuity. Professional services for businesses depend more on client demand and on specific capabilities. Training and business conditions produce steady work only when they meet demand that is actually there. Services are not one hiring channel.

## Nearby access and residence-based services can complement each other

The plans envisage residence-based assistance and a 15-minute employment-service network.[^5]

The State Council plan already in force calls for province-wide lists of public employment services, while encouraging distinctive local additions. It envisages an improved national employment information database, a China Employment public-service platform, and public employment services on campuses. For rural migrant workers it promotes basic public services at their usual residence. For flexible workers it calls for standardised casual-work markets.[^4]

Join those pieces up and a worker could get advice, a match or a training lead near home, instead of starting again at the next institution. A convenient service point still needs vacancies that are current, help that continues, and the same facts on every channel. Opening a new office is only one part of that capacity.

## Training, pay and job quality need to advance together

The State Council plan links job demand, skills training, assessment and employment services. It promotes project-based and order-based training, with training subsidies reaching employers and participants directly. It also calls for better minimum-wage adjustment mechanisms, skills-oriented pay systems, and enforcement of working-time and rest requirements.[^4]

Headcount is the easy measure. The harder ones are whether the training matched a real vacancy, whether a stronger skill brought better conditions, and whether the person was still in the job after being hired. Employers recruit and develop people more readily when the skill requirement is clear. Workers take a job, and stay, when the pay and the conditions can be predicted.

Local services, training and employer demand still have to meet. The aggregates show progress against the target. Whether an opening becomes work that lasts depends on how the plan is carried out.
