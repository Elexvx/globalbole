On November 12, 2026, [the UK Office for National Statistics (ONS) published its first quarterly estimate of gross domestic product (GDP) for Quarter 3 (July to September) 2026](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/grossdomesticproductgdp/bulletins/gdpfirstquarterlyestimateuk/julytoseptember2026), showing that UK real GDP increased by 0.7% quarter-on-quarter following 0.5% growth in Quarter 2 2026, standing 1.9% higher than the same quarter a year earlier[^1]. Real GDP per head rose 0.7% in Quarter 3 and 1.7% year-on-year, while monthly output expanded 0.2% in September after flat growth in August and a 0.4% gain in July[^1]. On the same day in Paris, [the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) released its flagship report on Digitalisation and Digital Transformation in Tax Administrations alongside an 86-page working paper on reviving productivity growth through business dynamism](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/forthcoming.html)[^2].

## AI and Cloud Computing Propel 4.4% Surge in Computer Programming and 4.6% Jump in Electronics Manufacturing

Across the output components of the national accounts, [13 of 20 subsectors expanded in Quarter 3 2026, led by a 0.9% quarterly increase in the services sector (+2.3% year-on-year)](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/grossdomesticproductgdp/bulletins/gdpfirstquarterlyestimateuk/julytoseptember2026)[^1]. Business-facing (non-consumer-facing) services grew 1.0%, outpacing consumer-facing services (+0.5%). Among the 14 services subsectors, administrative and support services rose 4.1% (driven by a 6.6% increase in rental and leasing), while information and communication climbed 2.1% on the back of a 4.4% surge in computer programming, consultancy, and related activities[^1]. Notably, the ONS highlighted that computer programming and consultancy has been the single largest driver of UK GDP growth across 2026, with survey returns confirming that many of the firms reporting the sharpest turnover gains are engaged in artificial intelligence and cloud computing activities[^1].

In industrial production, [overall output edged up 0.1% in Quarter 3 2026 (+1.5% year-on-year), reversing a 0.1% contraction in Quarter 2](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/grossdomesticproductgdp/bulletins/gdpfirstquarterlyestimateuk/julytoseptember2026)[^1]. While mining and quarrying (-2.2%), electricity, gas, steam, and air conditioning supply (-2.1%), and water and waste management (-1.5%) contracted, manufacturing grew 0.9% as 8 of 13 manufacturing subsectors expanded—led by a 4.6% jump in the manufacture of computer, electronic, and optical products[^1]. By contrast, construction output fell 0.7% in Quarter 3 (-1.7% year-on-year), weighed down by a 4.7% drop in private housing new work that offset a 1.5% increase in private housing repair and maintenance[^1].

## 1.7% Gross Fixed Capital Formation Expansion and OECD Digital Tax and Productivity Benchmarks

On the expenditure side, [real household final consumption grew 0.6% in Quarter 3 (+1.8% year-on-year) and government consumption rose 0.6%, while gross fixed capital formation (GFCF) advanced 1.7% on the quarter (+4.1% year-on-year)](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/grossdomesticproductgdp/bulletins/gdpfirstquarterlyestimateuk/julytoseptember2026) on stronger investment in machinery, equipment, and transport equipment, with business investment up 0.4% (+3.0% year-on-year)[^1]. Chained volume inventories excluding alignment adjustments fell by £2,043 million, and the UK trade deficit for goods and services held at 1.3% of nominal GDP as export volumes rose 1.6% (goods +2.2%, services +1.1%) and import volumes rose 1.8%[^1]. Nominal GDP grew 1.4% in Quarter 3 (+4.3% year-on-year) with the implied GDP deflator up 2.3% year-on-year, supported by a 1.3% increase in employee compensation and a 2.2% rise in corporate gross operating surplus (excluding alignment) to £173,415 million[^1].

The ONS evidence that AI software consultancy and electronic hardware manufacturing are driving UK output aligns closely with [the OECD's November 12 publications—Digitalisation and Digital Transformation in Tax Administrations and the Productivity Review of the Netherlands—as well as its forthcoming December 3 primer on competition and AI inputs for G7 jurisdictions](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/forthcoming.html)[^2]. Together, the UK national accounts data and OECD policy research indicate that enterprise AI deployment and capital equipment outlays are beginning to register measurable macroeconomic gains in late 2026, even as interest-sensitive residential construction continues to lag[^1][^2].
