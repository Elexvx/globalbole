On November 12, 2026, [leaders from across Europe convened in Dublin for the ninth meeting of the European Political Community (EPC), co-chaired by European Council President António Costa and Irish Taoiseach Micheál Martin](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/11/11/media-advisory-meeting-of-the-european-political-community-of-12-november-2026-and-the-informal-meeting-of-heads-of-state-or-government-of-13-november-2026/), ahead of an informal meeting of EU Heads of State or Government in Dublin on November 13[^1]. Simultaneously, [the World Meteorological Organization (WMO) released its Global Status of Multi-Hazard Early Warning Systems 2026 report](https://wmo.int/resources/publication-series/global-status-of-multi-hazard-early-warning-systems/gsmhews-2026)[^2], as WMO Secretary-General Celeste Saulo warned world leaders that while 70% of countries now report multi-hazard early warning systems, the strongest El Niño since at least 1950 is poised to push 2027 to a new global temperature record[^3].

## Dublin Back-to-Back Summits: Ninth European Political Community Meeting and EU Leaders' Informal Council

According to [the European Council media advisory and indicative programme](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/11/11/media-advisory-meeting-of-the-european-political-community-of-12-november-2026-and-the-informal-meeting-of-heads-of-state-or-government-of-13-november-2026/), the November 12 EPC summit in Dublin opened with plenary deliberations at 11:30 and concludes with a closing plenary at 17:15 and a joint press conference at 17:45 featuring Taoiseach Micheál Martin, European Council President António Costa, and Swiss Confederation President Guy Parmelin[^1]. On Friday, November 13, EU heads of state or government will remain in Dublin for an informal summit chaired by Costa and hosted by Martin alongside European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen[^1].

The European Council highlighted [competitiveness, foreign affairs, Eastern Europe, non-EU Western European cooperation, Ukraine security, and artificial intelligence as the core agenda pillars of the Dublin meetings](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/11/11/media-advisory-meeting-of-the-european-political-community-of-12-november-2026-and-the-informal-meeting-of-heads-of-state-or-government-of-13-november-2026/)[^1]. By pairing the broader pan-European EPC forum with an EU-27 leaders' working session the following morning, European policymakers aim to align industrial competitiveness, AI infrastructure governance, and cross-border security across both EU and non-EU European states[^1].

## WMO 2026 Early Warning Report: 136 Countries Covered, Six-Fold Lower Mortality, and 2027 El Niño Heat Risk

Alongside European diplomatic coordination, global meteorological authorities presented new evidence on disaster preparedness under the UN's Early Warnings for All (EW4All) initiative. According to [the Global Status of Multi-Hazard Early Warning Systems (MHEWS) 2026 report launched at COP31](https://wmo.int/resources/publication-series/global-status-of-multi-hazard-early-warning-systems/gsmhews-2026), 136 countries—70% of nations worldwide—reported having multi-hazard early warning systems in place as of August 2026, representing a 143% increase since 2015 and more than doubling over the past decade[^2][^3]. Where strong early warning systems operate, disaster mortality is six times lower; however, fewer than half of reporting countries demonstrate full capabilities across all four warning pillars, leaving severe observation gaps in vulnerable developing states[^2][^3].

In her [high-level intervention to world leaders](https://wmo.int/secretariat/celeste-saulo/cop31-intervention-state-of-climate-update-high-level-segment-of-leaders-summit), WMO Secretary-General Celeste Saulo detailed the physical drivers escalating global hazard risk: oceans—which absorb roughly 90% of excess heat—are on track for a tenth consecutive annual heat record while record sea levels threaten 900 million coastal residents; atmospheric carbon dioxide exceeds pre-industrial levels by more than 50%, methane has nearly tripled, nitrous oxide is up by over 25%, and Earth's energy imbalance has reached its highest level since monitoring began in 1960[^3]. With all glaciated regions losing ice for four consecutive years, 2025 ranking among the driest years for global rivers in 35 years, and January–September 2026 temperatures averaging 1.47°C above pre-industrial levels, Saulo warned that the arrival of the strongest El Niño since at least 1950 makes 2027 likely to set a new global temperature record—underscoring the urgency of sustained meteorological investment beyond the 2027 EW4All milestone[^2][^3].
