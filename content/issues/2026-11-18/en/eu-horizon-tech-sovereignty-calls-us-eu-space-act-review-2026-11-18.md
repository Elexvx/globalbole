On November 17, the European Commission Directorate-General for Communications Networks, Content and Technology (DG CONNECT) officially launched [two new funding calls totaling EUR 297 million across 12 topics under the Horizon Europe digital strand](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-launches-over-eu290-million-boost-eu-tech-sovereignty-and-beyond), targeting safe-by-design frontier artificial intelligence, ultra-low-power integrated photonics, energy-efficient AI data centers, and Web 4.0 hardware through March 18, 2027[^1]. On the same day, ahead of a November 19 vote in the European Parliament Committee on Industry, Research and Energy (ITRE), the U.S. Department of Commerce Office of Space Commerce (OSC) published its [EU Space Act Update and stakeholder consultation notice](https://space.commerce.gov/eu-space-act-update-november-17-2026/), announcing an interagency U.S. government review of the newly released ITRE compromise text and inviting U.S. commercial space operators to submit proposed redlines[^2].

## EUR 136 Million Allocated to Open Strategic Autonomy in Frontier AI, Robotics, and Integrated Photonics

According to the European Commission [Horizon Europe 2026-2027 Digital, Industry and Space funding announcement](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-launches-over-eu290-million-boost-eu-tech-sovereignty-and-beyond), the EUR 297 million package is structured across three strategic Destinations designed to close Europe deployment gap and reduce excessive external technological dependencies[^1]. The first Destination, focused on achieving open strategic autonomy in digital and enabling technologies, accounts for EUR 136 million, including EUR 107 million dedicated to four artificial intelligence topics: the EU Frontier AI Initiative for safe and computationally efficient models linked to the Resource for AI Science in Europe (RAISE), AI-driven industrial robotics system integration under the Apply AI framework, sectoral AI uptake acceleration, and international AI cooperation tailored for low- and middle-income partner countries[^1].

Within the same strategic autonomy pillar, the Commission allocated a further EUR 29 million across two hardware and foresight topics: research under the Photonics Partnership into advanced integrated photonic devices and circuits with extended functionality and ultra-low power consumption, alongside a European foresight and horizon-scanning forum for emerging digital materials and disruptive technologies[^1]. The Commission noted that the foresight mechanism directly aligns with the Draghi report and the EU Competitiveness Compass to ensure Europe secures a sovereign, future-proof industrial base before disruptive hardware paradigms mature[^1].

## EUR 112 Million Targets Sustainable Cloud-Edge AI Data Centers Alongside EUR 49 Million for Web 4.0 and Standards

To address the mounting electricity footprint of artificial intelligence workloads, the second Destination allocates EUR 112 million across three topics aimed at building an agile, secure single market and infrastructure for data services and trustworthy AI[^1]. These calls fund novel decentralized and federated AI data processing approaches across the cloud-to-edge continuum throughout the full model lifecycle, demand-side pilot demonstrators running on converged Telco Edge Cloud Connected Collaborative Computing (3C) infrastructure, and full-scale demonstration sites for energy-efficient and sustainable AI data processing in European data centers[^1].

The third Destination provides EUR 49 million across three human-centric digital and industrial technology topics: developing advanced extended-reality (XR) headsets, displays, wearables, haptic components, sensors, and multisensory chips under the Virtual Worlds Partnership; building an interoperable Virtual Worlds and Web 4.0 platform ecosystem across the telco-cloud-to-edge continuum; and supporting European stakeholder participation in international digital standardization bodies[^1]. All 12 topics opened for submissions on November 17 and will remain open until March 18, 2027[^1].

## U.S. Office of Space Commerce Solicits Industry Redlines Ahead of European Parliament Vote on EU Space Act

As Brussels expands funding across digital and space work programmes, transatlantic regulatory scrutiny of European space legislation is intensifying. In its [November 17 regulatory update on the European Union Space Act (EUSA)](https://space.commerce.gov/eu-space-act-update-november-17-2026/), the U.S. Department of Commerce Office of Space Commerce confirmed that the European Parliament ITRE Committee publicly released its legislative compromise text ahead of the committee scheduled vote on November 19[^2]. The Office emphasized that the parliamentary ITRE text is distinct from the updated non-consensus text released in October by the Irish Presidency of the Council of the European Union, and that both institutions must adopt their respective mandates before launching formal trialogue negotiations toward final enactment[^2].

Because the draft European Union Space Act establishes binding operational, safety, debris-mitigation, and market-access requirements affecting international satellite and launch providers serving European customers, the U.S. Department of Commerce is reviewing the ITRE text in coordination with the U.S. Department of State and partner federal agencies[^2]. The Office of Space Commerce formally invited U.S. commercial space stakeholders to submit technical feedback and proposed textual redlines on the ITRE committee draft via its dedicated NOAA space commerce mailbox to inform bilateral engagement prior to the EU trialogue phase[^2].
