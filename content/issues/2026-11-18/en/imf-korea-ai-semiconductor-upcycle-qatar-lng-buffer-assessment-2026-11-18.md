On November 17, the International Monetary Fund (IMF) published two macroeconomic mission statements across Asia and the Middle East: concluding its November 3-17 Article IV consultation in Seoul, the IMF announced in [Press Release No. 26/367 on the Republic of Korea](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/11/17/pr26367-republic-of-korea-imf-staff-completes-2026-aiv-mission) that the global artificial intelligence (AI) upcycle and record semiconductor exports are projected to lift South Korea real GDP growth from 1.1% in 2025 to 3.9% in 2026 and 3.3% in 2027[^1]. On the same day, concluding a November 3-12 staff visit to Doha, the Fund reported in [Press Release No. 26/368 on Qatar](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/11/17/pr26368-qatar-imf-staff-concludes-staff-visit) that although Strait of Hormuz shipping and hydrocarbon disruptions are projected to contract Qatar overall real GDP by about 14.5% in 2026, ample sovereign buffers and 3% H1 non-hydrocarbon growth have cushioned the shock ahead of a strong 2027-2031 LNG-led rebound[^2].

## Record AI Semiconductor Exports Boost Korean Growth and Fiscal Revenue as IMF Urges Policy Tightening

According to the [IMF Article IV mission concluding statement led by Mission Chief Corinne Delechat](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/11/17/pr26367-republic-of-korea-imf-staff-completes-2026-aiv-mission), surging semiconductor shipments have bolstered South Korean corporate earnings, fiscal receipts, and domestic AI investment, with income gains gradually spilling over into private consumption[^1]. Because the economy is operating above potential and domestic demand is strengthening, headline inflation is projected to reach 2.7% in 2026 and 2.3% in 2027—remaining above the Bank of Korea 2% target in the near term ahead of the IMF Executive Board tentative review of the Staff Report in January 2027[^1].

To contain aggregate demand pressures and mitigate sector-concentration risks without entrenching inflation, IMF staff recommended further monetary tightening paired with a somewhat more contractionary fiscal stance that preserves targeted support for vulnerable households and firms[^1]. The mission welcomed the creation of South Korea Future Response Fund as a countercyclical fiscal stabilization vehicle, praised proactive macroprudential measures to contain housing-market pressures and financial-sector vulnerabilities, and reiterated that exchange-rate flexibility should remain the primary shock absorber with foreign-exchange interventions limited to preventing disorderly market conditions[^1].

## Regional AI Diffusion Clusters Outside Seoul Recommended to Counter Aging and Productivity Gaps

The [IMF assessment of the Korean economy](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/11/17/pr26367-republic-of-korea-imf-staff-completes-2026-aiv-mission) cautioned that gains from the global AI boom remain heavily concentrated in the technology hardware sector, and that sustaining medium-term potential growth requires tackling structural headwinds including rapid population aging, regional disparities, and productivity gaps between large conglomerates and smaller firms[^1].

Staff advised prioritizing structural reforms that raise labor force participation, narrow wage disparities across worker categories, and strengthen social protection to unlock household consumption[^1]. To broaden AI diffusion across the wider economy, the IMF endorsed targeted public investment and the development of regional growth clusters outside the Seoul metropolitan area, alongside export-market diversification and accelerated renewable energy deployment to reduce external vulnerability[^1].

## Qatar Weathers 14.5% Hydrocarbon Contraction in 2026 with Strong Buffers and Projected 6% Medium-Term Rebound

In its [concluding statement on the staff visit to Qatar led by Nathan Porter](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/11/17/pr26368-qatar-imf-staff-concludes-staff-visit), the IMF noted that regional conflict and shipping disruptions through the Strait of Hormuz since early 2026 have curtailed hydrocarbon output and exports, causing real GDP to contract by an estimated 14.5% in 2026 after 1.8% growth in 2025[^2]. Nevertheless, non-hydrocarbon economic activity expanded by about 3% in the first half of 2026 as third-quarter air traffic and hotel occupancy returned to pre-conflict levels; headline inflation rose to 3% through August and is projected at 3.5% for 2026 (with core inflation at 1.5%), while Qatari banks maintain strong capital buffers with a low 3.2% nonperforming loan ratio and roughly 80% provisioning coverage[^2].

Looking ahead, IMF staff project Qatar economic growth to rebound strongly from 2027 and average roughly 6% annually during 2028-2031 as LNG trade normalizes and planned medium-term LNG capacity expansions come online, restoring double-digit current account surpluses and fiscal surpluses[^2]. Ahead of the formal Article IV consultation expected in the first half of 2027, the mission advised Qatari authorities to sustain Third National Development Strategy (NDS3) reforms, rationalize operational spending while protecting human-capital and infrastructure investment, and diversify trade and logistics routes through regional cooperation to mitigate maritime chokepoint risks[^2].
