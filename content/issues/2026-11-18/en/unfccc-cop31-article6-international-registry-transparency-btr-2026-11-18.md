On November 17 at the COP31 climate conference in Antalya, Turkiye, the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) Secretariat announced the [launch of the first version of the Paris Agreement Article 6.2 International Registry](https://unfccc.int/news/cop31-sees-launch-of-first-version-of-article-62-international-registry), providing sovereign accounting infrastructure for countries to issue, authorize, and track Internationally Transferred Mitigation Outcomes (ITMOs)[^1]. On the same day, addressing the COP31 Ministerial High-Level Dialogue on Transparency, UN Climate Change Executive Secretary Simon Stiell delivered [remarks on the Enhanced Transparency Framework](https://unfccc.int/news/transparency-is-essential-to-ensuring-climate-action-is-grounded-in-solid-evidence-un-climate-chief), highlighting that the latest synthesis report covering the first 137 Biennial Transparency Reports (BTRs) confirms 61 Parties representing roughly 66% of 2020 global greenhouse gas emissions have made progress toward their Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs)[^2].

## Article 6.2 International Registry Goes Live on CorTenX to Track ITMO Issuance, Authorization, and Transfers

According to the [UNFCCC announcement on the Article 6.2 International Registry](https://unfccc.int/news/cop31-sees-launch-of-first-version-of-article-62-international-registry), the platform was mandated by the Conference of the Parties serving as the meeting of the Parties to the Paris Agreement (CMA) and is operated by the UN Climate Change Secretariat for countries that do not have or cannot access a national registry[^1]. Participating governments can also request registry services to issue mitigation outcomes as accounting units and hold them until their first international transfer, at which point they become ITMOs recorded in the international registry[^1].

Demonstrated at a COP31 launch event in Antalya, the first version enables countries to open dedicated national sections and accounts, record mitigation activities, issue mitigation outcomes linked to formal authorizations, and record the transfer, acquisition, use, and cancellation of ITMOs[^1]. UNFCCC Mitigation Director James Grabert noted that software development began in January 2026 and reached a minimum viable product in May 2026 in collaboration with Trovio (deployed on its CorTenX registry platform) and EY; staged country onboarding will begin with an early-2027 workshop, training, and system testing, while future releases will add reporting capabilities and full interoperability with national registries and the Paris Agreement Crediting Mechanism registry[^1].

## Synthesis of 137 Biennial Transparency Reports Shows 61 Parties Covering 66% of Global Emissions Advancing on NDCs

Alongside the carbon-market registry launch, UN Climate Change Executive Secretary Simon Stiell underscored in his [statement to the COP31 Ministerial High-Level Dialogue on Transparency](https://unfccc.int/news/transparency-is-essential-to-ensuring-climate-action-is-grounded-in-solid-evidence-un-climate-chief) that the Paris Agreement Enhanced Transparency Framework (ETF) represents a core political commitment by governments to demonstrate both concrete implementation and remaining policy gaps[^2]. Stiell congratulated Colombia, Turkiye, and Kazakhstan for submitting their second Biennial Transparency Reports ahead of the year-end deadline and commended Least Developed Countries and Small Island Developing States for submitting BTRs at their own discretion despite resource constraints[^2].

Citing the newly compiled BTR Synthesis Report across the first 137 submissions, Stiell reported that 61 Parties—accounting for approximately 66% of global emissions in 2020—have demonstrated measurable progress toward their NDC targets[^2]. While confirming that Paris Agreement implementation is well underway, Stiell cautioned that current progress remains far short of keeping global temperature goals within reach and urged ministers to back national transparency institutions with adequate domestic budgets[^2].

## Enhanced Transparency Data Feeds Second Global Stocktake as Carbon Markets Shift from Rulemaking to Delivery

Stiell emphasized in his [Antalya transparency remarks](https://unfccc.int/news/transparency-is-essential-to-ensuring-climate-action-is-grounded-in-solid-evidence-un-climate-chief) that the completed first cycle of the Enhanced Transparency Framework serves as a primary evidence input into the second Global Stocktake, which officially launches at COP31 in Antalya[^2]. The UNFCCC Secretariat pledged to expand technical capacity-building for developing Parties and maintain the Together For Transparency initiative to engage non-state actors in climate data verification[^2].

Taken together with the [Article 6.2 International Registry deployment](https://unfccc.int/news/cop31-sees-launch-of-first-version-of-article-62-international-registry)—which Trovio CEO Jon Deane and EY Partner Christophe Lumsden described as marking the transition of Paris Agreement carbon markets from years of rulemaking to operational delivery—the convergence of verified national BTR inventories and live ITMO tracking provides countries with end-to-end accounting infrastructure to scale cross-border clean energy and industrial mitigation investment[^1][^2].
