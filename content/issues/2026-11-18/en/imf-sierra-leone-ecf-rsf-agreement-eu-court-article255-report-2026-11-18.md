On November 17, the International Monetary Fund (IMF) announced in [Press Release No. 26/365 on Sierra Leone](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/11/17/pr26365-sierra-leone-imf-staff-level-agreement-fourth-review-extended-credit-facility-arrangement) that IMF staff and Sierra Leonean authorities reached a staff-level agreement on the fourth review under the Extended Credit Facility (ECF) and the first review under the Resilience and Sustainability Facility (RSF), which will make SDR 57.9 million (about USD 78.2 million) available subject to Executive Board approval and completion of prior actions[^1]. On the same day in Brussels, the Council of the European Union published the [eighth activity report of the Article 255 judicial appointments advisory panel](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/11/17/appointments-to-the-eu-court-of-justice-eighth-activity-report-of-the-article-255-panel-published/), documenting 70 opinions delivered between March 2022 and February 2026 on candidates for the Court of Justice and the General Court—including 9 unfavorable opinions—all of which were unanimously followed by EU Member State governments[^2].

## IMF Agreement Unlocks USD 31.5 Million in ECF and USD 46.7 Million in RSF Financing as Sierra Leone Tightens Policy

Following discussions in Freetown from October 28 to November 10 led by Mission Chief Jeta Menkulasi, the [IMF staff-level agreement with Sierra Leone](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/11/17/pr26365-sierra-leone-imf-staff-level-agreement-fourth-review-extended-credit-facility-arrangement) covers the fourth review of the ECF approved on October 31, 2024 and the first review of the RSF approved on June 18, 2026, unlocking SDR 23.333 million (about USD 31.5 million) under the ECF and SDR 34.567 million (about USD 46.7 million) under the RSF upon Board approval[^1]. While real GDP growth is projected at 4.0% in 2026 and 4.4% in 2027, high global oil prices have strained foreign-exchange reserves and pushed headline inflation to 17.1% in September and an estimated 19% by year-end before a projected decline to single digits by end-2027[^1].

With interest payments absorbing roughly one-third of tax revenue and third-quarter spending overruns eroding earlier fiscal gains, Sierra Leonean authorities phased out temporary fuel subsidies, curbed non-priority outlays, and committed to a 2027 budget that safeguards the school-feeding program, publishes independent domestic arrears audit findings, and initiates an arrears-clearance strategy[^1]. Simultaneously, the Bank of Sierra Leone raised its policy rate to 17.25%, closed the Union Trust Bank resolution funding gap, and completed RSF milestones assessing climate-related fiscal risks and bank lending disclosures by location and sector[^1].

## EU Article 255 Judicial Panel Reviewed 70 Court of Justice and General Court Candidates with Nine Rejections

In European institutional governance, the Council of the European Union released its [press announcement on the eighth activity report of the Article 255 panel](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/11/17/appointments-to-the-eu-court-of-justice-eighth-activity-report-of-the-article-255-panel-published/), covering the panel work from March 1, 2022 through February 28, 2026[^2]. Established under Article 255 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU), the seven-member independent panel assesses the suitability of national nominees for Judge and Advocate-General at the Court of Justice and the General Court before Member State governments finalize judicial appointments[^2].

During the four-year reporting period, the panel delivered 70 formal opinions on judicial candidates, including 33 opinions regarding first terms of office that required extensive written scrutiny and in-person hearings before the panel[^2]. Nine of the 70 opinions—representing 27.3% of first-term nominations and 12.9% of all opinions—were unfavorable, and EU Member State governments followed the panel recommendations in 100% of cases[^2].

## Seven-Member Panel Led by Allan Rosas Enforces Six Treaty Criteria for Judicial Independence and Legal Competence

As detailed in the [Council of the EU release](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/11/17/appointments-to-the-eu-court-of-justice-eighth-activity-report-of-the-article-255-panel-published/), the 2022-2026 panel was chaired by former EU Court of Justice Judge Allan Rosas and included former Irish Chief Justice Frank Clarke, Estonian Supreme Court Judge and former European Court of Human Rights Judge Julia Laffranque, former EU General Court Judge Maria Eugenia Martins de Nazare Ribeiro, Czech Supreme Administrative Court Vice-President Barbara Porizkova, former Italian Constitutional Court President Silvana Sciarra, and former EU Court of Justice President Vassilios Skouris[^2].

The panel evaluates candidates holistically across six treaty-grounded criteria: legal capabilities, professional experience, ability to perform judicial duties independently, language proficiency, capacity to work collaboratively within a multilingual environment representing diverse legal systems, and guarantees of independence, impartiality, probity, and integrity[^2]. Supported by a secretariat within the General Secretariat of the Council, the eighth activity report provides public transparency into how the European Union safeguards rule-of-law standards across its highest courts while preserving the confidentiality of individual candidate deliberations[^2].
