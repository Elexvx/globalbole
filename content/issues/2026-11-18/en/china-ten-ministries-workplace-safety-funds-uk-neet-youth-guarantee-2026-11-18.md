On November 18, ten central regulatory bodies in China—led by the Ministry of Emergency Management, the Ministry of Finance, and the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology—jointly published [Guiding Opinions Yingji [2026] No. 98 on Strengthening Enterprise Workplace Safety Fund Management for Preventive Governance](https://www.mem.gov.cn/gk/zfxxgkpt/fdzdgknr/202611/t20261118_739805.shtml), setting out 15 regulatory measures across five sections to enforce full budgetary safety extraction, prioritize intelligent monitoring, refine negative lists, and incorporate safety spending into central annual inspections[^1]. Concurrently on November 17, the UK Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) issued its [official government response to Alan Milburn independent Young People and Work Review](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/the-independent-young-people-and-work-review-what-it-means-for-young-people), addressing why roughly 1 million young people aged 16 to 24 are not in education, employment, or training (NEET) by unveiling locally led one-stop youth services and a new Opportunity and Support Guarantee starting next year[^2].

## China Ten-Ministry Directive Prioritizes Intelligent Safety Monitoring, Central SOE Pooling, and Industry Negative Lists

Issued jointly by the Ministry of Emergency Management, Ministry of Finance, MIIT, Ministry of Housing and Urban-Rural Development, Ministry of Transport, Ministry of Water Resources, National Energy Administration, SASTIND, National Railway Administration, and National Mine Safety Administration, [Document Yingji [2026] No. 98](https://www.mem.gov.cn/gk/zfxxgkpt/fdzdgknr/202611/t20261118_739805.shtml) operationalizes China Workplace Safety Law by shifting industrial safety governance toward upfront prevention[^1]. Enterprises in regulated sectors must compile annual workplace safety fund extraction and expenditure plans within their corporate budgets and immediately scale up safety allocations when expanding production capacity; meanwhile, central state-owned enterprise (SOE) parent groups bearing group-wide safety accountability are authorized to centrally pool and allocate a defined proportion of safety funds extracted by wholly owned and controlled subsidiaries, backed by regular internal audits and mandatory reporting through the national Enterprise Workplace Safety Fund Statistical Reporting System[^1].

To ensure funds directly mitigate operational hazards, the 15-clause directive requires enterprises to maintain traceable ledgers and prioritize expenditures on statutory safety standardization, hazard identification and remediation, intelligent safety automation, real-time monitoring systems, and statutory workplace safety liability insurance[^1]. Regulators will jointly formulate industry-specific negative lists to eliminate ambiguous or off-scope spending and encourage non-mandated commercial enterprises and production-oriented public institutions to benchmark annual safety investments against comparable industrial extraction rates[^1].

## Financial Audit Enforcement, CSR Disclosure Mandates, and Central Safety Inspection Accountability

To strengthen enforcement quality, the [ten-ministry guidance](https://www.mem.gov.cn/gk/zfxxgkpt/fdzdgknr/202611/t20261118_739805.shtml) instructs local emergency management and sectoral authorities to embed safety fund extraction and usage into annual law-enforcement inspection plans and industry safety standardization evaluations, while mobilizing professional financial and accounting auditors to detect misappropriation, under-extraction, fraudulent accounting, or off-scope spending[^1]. Regulators will intensify joint enforcement actions and publicly expose typical violation cases, particularly where insufficient safety investment contributes to industrial accidents[^1].

Alongside penalties, the framework establishes positive credit incentives and social oversight: enterprises demonstrating exemplary safety fund execution will receive credit upgrades, honor recognitions, and regulatory service support, while covered enterprises must disclose safety fund extraction and utilization figures within their corporate social responsibility (CSR) reports[^1]. At the national level, the Ministry of Emergency Management will formally incorporate enterprise safety fund oversight into the annual Central Workplace Safety Assessment and Inspection Patrols[^1].

## UK Responds to Alan Milburn Review on One Million NEET Youth with Local Hubs and Opportunity Guarantee

In parallel labor-market policy action in Europe, the UK Department for Work and Pensions published its [response to the independent Young People and Work Review led by Alan Milburn](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/the-independent-young-people-and-work-review-what-it-means-for-young-people), which examined why approximately 1 million young people aged 16 to 24 in the United Kingdom are currently not in education, employment, or training (NEET)[^2]. Drawing on consultations with health, business, and policy experts as well as young people themselves, the Milburn Review called for earlier intervention across education, healthcare, and the benefits system to prevent youth economic inactivity before disengagement becomes entrenched[^2].

Endorsing the review core principles, the UK government announced that existing benefit entitlements remain unchanged while immediate steps begin for a new youth settlement starting in 2027: locally led youth services shaped by mayors and local leaders will integrate employment, health, skills, and youth support in a single hub, while a new Opportunity and Support Guarantee will ensure every young person has access to a college place, training, an apprenticeship, high-quality work experience, or a job alongside a permanent safety net for those unable to work[^2]. A comprehensive White Paper and enabling legislation will be published in 2027 in alignment with the Timms Review, Education and Special Educational Needs and Disabilities (SEND) reform, mental health reform, and the Rewiring the State program[^2].
