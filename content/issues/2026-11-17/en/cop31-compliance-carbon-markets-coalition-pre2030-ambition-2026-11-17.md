## Brazil, China, and the EU Steer 13-Member Carbon Market Coalition Toward 2027 Work Programme

Launched one year earlier at COP30 in Belém as a voluntary, member-led intergovernmental forum, the Open Coalition on Compliance Carbon Markets seeks to advance compliance emissions trading systems as cost-effective policy instruments for fulfilling Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) under the Paris Agreement. Convened in Antalya with Brazil serving as Chair and China and the European Union as Co-Chairs, the High-Level Ministerial Meeting confirmed that the Coalition has grown to 13 Members and 34 Observers as of November 16, 2026. By adopting the OCCCM COP31 Declaration, ministers inaugurated dedicated Working Groups to promote technical convergence and long-term interoperability while respecting distinct national regulatory designs[^1].

Kurt Vandenberghe, Director-General for Climate Action at the European Commission and OCCCM Co-Chair, stated that the Coalition's rapid expansion demonstrates sustained multilateral commitment to carbon pricing and investment mobilization. He noted that establishing benchmarks for effective compliance pricing and high-quality international carbon credits is a central European legislative priority for 2027, as the European Commission prepares a formal proposal defining how high-integrity international carbon credits can count toward the EU's 2040 climate target[^1].

## COP31 Pre-2030 Ambition Ministerial Round Table Reviews Sharm el-Sheikh Mitigation Work Programme

Complementing carbon market coordination, the COP31 Presidency convened the annual High-Level Ministerial Round Table (HLMRT) on pre-2030 ambition on Tuesday, November 17, 2026, from 15:00 to 18:00 (UTC+3) in Meeting Room 19, Zone D3 in Antalya, pursuant to Decisions 1/CMA.3 and 4/CMA.4. Co-facilitated by German Federal Minister for the Environment, Climate Action, Nature Conservation and Nuclear Safety Carsten Schneider and Indian Minister for Environment, Forest and Climate Change Bhupender Yadav, the session opened with remarks from COP31 President Murat Kurum, COP31 President of Negotiations Chris Bowen, and UNFCCC Executive Secretary Simon Stiell[^2].

During the scene-setting segment, Sharm el-Sheikh Mitigation Ambition and Implementation Work Programme (MWP) co-chairs Angela Churie Kallhauge and Gao Xiang presented the consolidated 2025 and 2026 annual reports detailing high-impact clean power, grid modernization, and industrial decarbonization solutions. Across the subsequent 130-minute ministerial exchange, delegations addressed three guiding questions: national implementation bottlenecks and enabling conditions for climate policies, core mitigation outcomes sought at COP31, and concrete improvements and future timeframe extensions for the MWP under consideration by CMA 8[^2].
