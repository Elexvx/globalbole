## European Advisors Warn Against Delegating Model Safety Evaluation to Commercial Developers

Although the seven-member Group of Chief Scientific Advisors and the European Group on Ethics have previously issued separate opinions on artificial intelligence in scientific research, crisis management, and neuro-technology governance, their joint statement—titled The Next Phase of Artificial Intelligence: Governing for Safety, Security and Human Agency—marks an unusual coordinated intervention. The advisors warn that as autonomous and general-purpose systems become deeply embedded across critical data processing and public administration, traditional regulatory guardrails no longer suffice. They caution that insufficient institutional enforcement capacity or external pressure to delay or dilute European legislation could erode public safeguards[^1].

To prevent regulatory lag, the two advisory bodies set out four core recommendations anchored in the EU Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), the 2025 General-Purpose AI Code of Practice, and the proposed Cloud and AI Development Act (COM(2026) 502 final). First, safety, security, and meaningful human agency must remain non-negotiable prerequisites for system deployment. Second, the pace of model development and rollout should be calibrated directly to verified degrees of potential harm. Third, the European Union must build its own independent technical capacity to evaluate frontier models rather than delegating safety assessments to commercial developers. Fourth, regulators should establish a continuous-learning evaluation ecosystem whose empirical testing methodologies evolve in lockstep with model capabilities[^1].

## FDA Clarifies Executive Order 14434 Scope and Preserves Statutory Filing Standards

In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration detailed how federal medical product oversight will implement Executive Order 14434, Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence, signed on September 29, 2026. Section 3(a) of the directive instructs federal executive departments and agencies, to the maximum extent permitted by law, to replace "Artificial Intelligence" and "AI" with "Super Intelligence" and "SI" across official correspondence, websites, reports, and non-statutory policy documents, while tying the definition directly to the existing statutory scope of artificial intelligence under 15 U.S.C. 9401(3)[^2].

Beginning November 16, 2026, newly issued public-facing FDA communications adopt the "Super Intelligence" and "SI" terminology, whereas existing guidance documents, Federal Register notices, electronic submission templates, and regulatory databases will be updated gradually during scheduled maintenance cycles or future substantive revisions. Crucially for regulated industries across pharmaceuticals, biologics, medical devices, food, and veterinary products, the agency confirmed that existing rules and guidance remain in full effect as written, statutory citations to "artificial intelligence" will continue to be quoted verbatim, and the executive order imposes no new obligations on sponsors, applicants, or manufacturers regarding established product names, trade names, or labeling[^2].
