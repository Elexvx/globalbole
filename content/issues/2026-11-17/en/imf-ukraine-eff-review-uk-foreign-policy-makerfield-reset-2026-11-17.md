## IMF Ukraine Mission Reports Strong Quantitative Compliance but Projects USD 32–34 Billion Residual Gap in 2027

According to IMF mission chief Gavin Gray, Ukrainian authorities met all end-September Quantitative Performance Criteria (QPCs) and completed four of six structural benchmarks through end-October (two on time and two with delay), while advancing the remaining two benchmarks covering the independence of the energy regulator (NEURC) and tax-code amendments on transfer pricing and interest deductibility. However, reflecting intensified attacks on energy, logistics, and export infrastructure and the baseline assumption that the war will continue through 2027, IMF staff revised Ukraine's real GDP growth forecast down markedly to -0.5% in 2026 and -1.0% in 2027, estimating a residual financing gap of USD 32–34 billion in 2027 and USD 17 billion in 2028[^1].

Although Ukrainian bank lending grew 31.6% year-on-year in the second quarter of 2026 with liquid balance sheets and low non-performing loans, infrastructure damage is heightening credit risks and insurance payout demands. IMF staff stressed that Ukraine's 2027 budget must strictly restrain non-defense spending, reform the simplified tax regime to curb the informal economy, align virtual-assets legislation with EU standards, and establish targeted social protection for vulnerable households before liberalizing energy markets and reforming public service obligations (PSOs)[^1].

## UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham Applies the "Makerfield Test" to Ukraine Support, Commonwealth Critical Minerals, and the UK-EU Summit

Delivering his first major foreign-policy speech four months after taking office, Prime Minister Andy Burnham defined the "Makerfield Test"—judging international commitments by whether they lower cost-of-living pressures and create secure jobs in Britain's least affluent deindustrialized communities. Reaffirming unwavering British backing for Ukraine ahead of Thursday's Coalition of the Willing meeting in Berlin and ongoing Abu Dhabi talks, Burnham noted that a Ukrainian-founded drone manufacturer announced a UK production expansion last week creating high-skilled British jobs in engineering, AI, and advanced manufacturing. In the Middle East, he pointed to a twelve-nation coalition backing Britain's halt to trade with illegal West Bank settlements alongside diplomatic efforts to end the war with Iran and reopen the Strait of Hormuz[^2].

Working with Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband, Burnham outlined resets with the Commonwealth—offering a structured 2027 dialogue on historical injustices without reparations, debt and development finance initiatives for Britain's 2027 G20 Presidency, and engagement with the Commonwealth critical minerals taskforce spanning 2.7 billion people, USD 16 trillion in GDP, and up to 80% of certain critical mineral mining—and with the European Union. Citing Office for Budget Responsibility (OBR) estimates that hard Brexit reduced UK GDP by 4% (and external estimates up to 8%), Burnham stated that Britain cannot turn down a practical Brussels summit deal negotiated on foundations laid by Keir Starmer that could add up to 0.4% to GDP, lower food and electricity bills, and cut business red tape, with legislation to be presented to Parliament next week and a "Manchester Compact on AI" targeted for the November 2027 G20 Leaders' Summit[^2].
