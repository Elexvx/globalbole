## European Commission Protects EUR 23.2 Billion Cold-Rolled Steel Market with Systemic Trade-Defense Precedent

Under the definitive measures announced in Brussels on November 16, 2026, the European Commission set anti-dumping duty rates on cold-rolled flat steel imports at 9.5% for India, 27.9% for Japan, 3% to 7.8% for Türkiye, 16% for Vietnam, and 20.7% to 27% for other investigated origins. Cold-rolled flat steel is a foundational input across European manufacturing, representing an internal EU market valued at approximately EUR 23.2 billion, with domestic production facilities across 12 Member States employing more than 8,600 workers. Imports from the investigated origins totaled EUR 282 million from India, EUR 240 million from Türkiye, EUR 180 million from Japan, EUR 125 million from Vietnam, and EUR 345 million from other covered producers[^1].

The Commission emphasized that the investigation carries systemic significance across EU trade-defense policy beyond the steel sector because it is the first probe in which Brussels formally established "Particular Market Situations" in exporting economies. Under EU anti-dumping law, when domestic market distortions prevent a valid comparison between domestic sales prices and export prices, investigators may construct normal value rather than rely on local prices. Specifically, the Commission found that government measures in India distorted domestic iron-ore input pricing, while steel overcapacity spillovers depressed domestic prices and margins in another investigated market, prompting the Commission to construct normal values to capture the full dumping margin[^1].

## CSRC 23rd International Advisory Council Focuses on Long-Term Capital Channels and AI Supervisory Resilience

In Shanghai, the China Securities Regulatory Commission convened the 23rd meeting of its International Advisory Council (established in June 2004) on November 13, 2026, publishing the official summary on November 16. Chaired by Sir Howard Davies with Laura Cha as Vice Chair alongside 14 international council members, the meeting brought together CSRC Chairman Wu Qing, Vice Chairman Liu Haoling, and representatives from the People's Bank of China, the National Financial Regulatory Administration, and the Shanghai Municipal Government under the theme "China's Capital Market Toward 2030: Building an Inclusive Ecosystem, Enhancing Market Resilience, and Improving Global Governance"[^2].

Reviewing regulatory progress alongside the 15th Five-Year Plan framework, council members advised that China's capital market should prioritize four structural pillars: expanding the sources, channels, and entry mechanisms for medium- and long-term institutional capital to anchor market stability; smoothing the full investment-and-exit cycle of private equity funds to strengthen synergy between private capital and public equity markets; assessing both opportunities and risks from artificial intelligence while upgrading intelligent regulatory supervision and financial infrastructure security; and deepening rules-based institutional opening-up to contribute to global financial governance[^2].
