Global diagnostic standardization in public health and industrial supply-chain traceability advanced in parallel on November 16, 2026. Ahead of World AMR Awareness Week (November 18–24), the [World Health Organization (WHO)](https://www.who.int/news/item/16-11-2026-more-countries-have-antimicrobial-resistance-plans-but-patients-still-lack-testing-and-treatment) released findings from its 2026 Tracking Antimicrobial Resistance Country Self-Assessment Survey (TrACSS) across 183 Member States alongside two new clinical laboratory toolkits[^1], while the [European Commission Directorate-General for Internal Market, Industry, Entrepreneurship and SMEs](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/news/digital-product-passport-registry-technical-guidance-available-2026-11-16_en) published the complete technical integration specifications for the European Union Digital Product Passport (DPP) Registry[^2].

## WHO TrACSS 2026 Survey: 173 Countries Have AMR Plans, Yet Only 22% Report Full Funding and Execution

Completed by 183 countries—representing 94% of WHO Member States—under the Quadripartite monitoring mechanism linking FAO, UNEP, WHO, and WOAH, the 2026 TrACSS assessment shows that 173 countries now have national antimicrobial resistance (AMR) action plans, more than double the 79 countries recorded in 2017. However, only 22% of participating nations report having both dedicated funding and full operational implementation. With roughly one in six laboratory-confirmed bacterial infections worldwide exhibiting antibiotic resistance in 2023, WHO Director-General Dr Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus warned that unfulfilled plans leave patients unprotected across surgery, childbirth, and oncology care[^1].

The survey pinpoints clinical microbiology infrastructure as a severe bottleneck: only 36% of countries possess a national reference laboratory capable of susceptibility testing for priority bacterial and fungal pathogens, far below the 2030 global target of 80%. Furthermore, only 63% of countries maintain clinical guidelines for appropriate antimicrobial prescribing, and just over half operate national surveillance systems tracking antibiotic consumption and resistance trends. To bridge these operational gaps under the AMR Diagnostic Initiative and the 2026 campaign theme "One Health, one action: Prevent AMR now", WHO issued a prioritized catalogue of essential bacterial and fungal diagnostic tests together with a standardized toolkit of laboratory standard operating procedures (SOPs)[^1].

## European Commission Publishes OpenAPI and Semantic Validation Guidelines for the DPP Registry

In parallel, the European Commission released a comprehensive technical documentation package to help economic operators, software developers, and data service providers connect to the EU Digital Product Passport (DPP) Registry, with initial onboarding focused on the large industrial and electric-vehicle battery sector. Under the European single-market architecture, the central DPP Registry serves as an EU-wide index storing unique product identifiers and core registration metadata, while detailed product-level lifecycle and compliance records remain decentralized on infrastructure controlled and updated directly by economic operators[^2].

The technical package comprises four operational components: OpenAPI specifications and application programming interface (API) integration manuals covering authentication and passport registration; Service Account documentation enabling secure machine-to-machine (M2M) communication with Registry services; self-hosted authentication guidelines detailing how firms validate and secure access to decentralized DPP repositories; and semantic data resources containing machine-readable schemas and validation rules for battery passport attributes. The European Commission has also opened a dedicated sandbox testing environment as Registry features roll out progressively[^2].
